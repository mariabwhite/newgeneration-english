⚡ Two traps to watch

Trap 1 · special nouns for people. Some nationalities have a special noun for the person: 🇬🇧 an Englishman, 🇫🇷 a Frenchman, 🇳🇱 a Dutchman, 🇸🇪 a Swede, 🇵🇱 a Pole, 🇹🇷 a Turk, 🇪🇸 a Spaniard. In everyday talk you can just say «I'm British / French / Dutch / Swedish / Polish / Turkish / Spanish».
Trap 2 · Portuguese in TWO countries. Not only 🇵🇹 Portugal — also 🇧🇷 Brazil. And Brazilians will correct you fast if you speak Spanish to them!

📖 How to describe a flag

Use the pattern: The flag of X is + colours + pattern. Common patterns: three horizontal stripes · three vertical stripes · a cross · a circle · a star · a crescent.
🇮🇹
The flag of Italy has three vertical stripes: green, white and red. The green is on the LEFT.
🇫🇷
The flag of France also has three vertical stripes — but the colours are blue, white and red. Blue is on the left.
🇩🇪
The German flag has three horizontal stripes: black, red and gold. Top to bottom.
🇯🇵
Japan has a very simple flag: a big red circle on a white square. It represents the rising sun.
🇬🇷
Greece has nine horizontal stripes — five blue and four white — and a white cross on a blue square in the top-left corner.
🇹🇷
The flag of Turkey is red, with a white crescent moon and a white star next to it.
🇸🇪
The Swedish flag has a yellow cross on a blue background. All Scandinavian flags use a cross like this.
🇧🇷
Brazil has a green background, a yellow diamond in the middle, and inside the diamond — a blue circle with stars.

Reading · Julia's postcards

📖 7 postcards · 5 questions
A little world tour with real clues in every postcard
Julia · around the world in 7 postcards

🇮🇹 «Ciao, mum! I'm in Rome. My friend Marco is Italian — of course! He speaks Italian so fast, but he teaches me two words a day. Yesterday we ate pizza in a small café.»

🇯🇵 «Hi from Tokyo! It is very big. My host sister Yuki is Japanese. She speaks Japanese and a little English. She showed me how to write my name in Japanese letters.»

🇧🇷 «Hola from Brasilia! Well — actually it's olá, not hola, because in Brazil people speak Portuguese, not Spanish. That surprised me. The currency here is the real.»

🇬🇷 «Kalimera from Athens! Sophia is Greek. She showed me the letters of the Greek alphabet. Some of them look like maths! I bought feta cheese and olives at the market.»

🇹🇷 «Merhaba from Istanbul. My friend Emre is Turkish. We drank tiny cups of tea. The capital of Turkey is Ankara, not Istanbul — I always forget!»

🇮🇳 «Namaste from New Delhi! Priya is Indian. Today we visited the Taj Mahal — well, actually it's in Agra, so we took a train. Priya speaks Hindi and perfect English.»

🇲🇽 «Hola from Mexico City! Diego is Mexican and speaks Spanish. He took me for tacos with three kinds of hot sauce. I could not stop drinking water.»

— Love, Julia

Airport dialogues · what nationality are you?

Three tiny conversations at passport control

JuliaI am from Russia. I am Russian.
OfficerHow long are you staying?
JuliaFive days. I am a tourist.
OfficerEnjoy your visit!
🇮🇹 At Rome airport
OfficerBuongiorno. Passport, please.
JuliaHere you are.
OfficerWhere are you from?
JuliaI am from Russia. And I speak a little Italian.
OfficerMolto bene! Welcome to Italy.
🇯🇵 At Tokyo airport
OfficerKonnichiwa. Are you here for business or holiday?
JuliaHoliday. I am from Russia. I am Russian.
OfficerDo you speak Japanese?
JuliaOnly two words: konnichiwa and arigato.

Fun facts · true / false / not stated

🧠 8 questions
All facts are about the countries above · look back if you need to
1«Portuguese is spoken in both Portugal AND Brazil.»
2«The capital of Australia is Sydney.»
The capital of Australia is Canberra, not Sydney. Common mistake!
3«The Japanese flag has a red circle on a white background.»
4«Russians pay in dollars.»
Russia uses the ruble, not the dollar.
5«Egypt is on the continent of Africa.»
6«More people live in China than in India.»
Population numbers are not in this lesson. Not Stated.
7«France, Germany, Italy and Spain all use the euro.»
8«In Brazil, people say hola to say hello.»
Brazilians speak Portuguese and say olá, not hola (that's Spanish).
