⚡ Two traps to watch

Trap 1 · special nouns for people. Some nationalities have a SPECIAL noun for the person: 🇵🇱 a Pole, 🇹🇷 a Turk, 🇪🇸 a Spaniard. In everyday talk you can just say «I'm Polish / Turkish / Spanish».
Trap 2 · Spanish in MANY countries. Not only 🇪🇸 Spain — also 🇲🇽 Mexico and 🇵🇪 Peru! There is no such language as «Peruvian» or «Mexican». Same trap with Portuguese: it's spoken in 🇵🇹 Portugal AND 🇧🇷 Brazil.

📚 3 patterns · adjective → one person → the whole nation

Every nationality word has to work in three roles: adjective (a Russian book), one person (a Russian, an Englishman), the whole nation as a group (the Russians, the English). English splits them into three patterns.
Pattern A · endings -an · -ian — the noun for the person is just the adjective + -s for plural
AdjectiveOne personMany peopleThe nation as a whole
German ★a GermanGermansthe Germans
Italian ★an ItalianItaliansthe Italians
Mexican ★a MexicanMexicansthe Mexicans
Peruvian ★a PeruvianPeruviansthe Peruvians
Greek ★a GreekGreeksthe Greeks
same pattern (bonus)
Americanan AmericanAmericansthe Americans
Russiana RussianRussiansthe Russians
Braziliana BrazilianBraziliansthe Brazilians

🎯 Drill after Pattern A · 3 questions

A1Hans is ___ from Berlin.
One person → a German (adjective + a). Same for Italian, Mexican, Greek, Peruvian, Russian.
A2Many ___ love football.
Many people → Italians (with -s). Pattern A always takes -s in plural.
A3___ celebrate the Day of the Dead every autumn.
The whole nation → the Mexicans (the + nation).
Pattern B · endings -ese · -ss — same word for one person AND many people (no -s!)
AdjectiveOne personMany peopleThe nation as a whole
Portuguese ★a Portuguese(many) Portuguesethe Portuguese
Chinesea Chinese(many) Chinesethe Chinese
Japanesea Japanese(many) Japanesethe Japanese
Swissa Swiss(many) Swissthe Swiss

🎯 Drill after Pattern B · 2 questions

B1In this room there are ___ students from Lisbon.
Pattern B: no plural -s. Say two Portuguese, two Chinese, two Japanese.
B2___ love football and pastel de nata.
Whole nation → the Portuguese. There is no «Portugueses» — never add -s here!
Pattern C · endings -sh · -ch · -tch — the adjective and the nation are the same, BUT for one person you need a SPECIAL noun

Englishan Englishman · EnglishwomanEnglishmen · EnglishwomenNOT «an English»
Frencha Frenchman · FrenchwomanFrenchmen · FrenchwomenNOT «a French»
Dutcha Dutchman · DutchwomanDutchmen · DutchwomenNOT «a Dutch»

⚡ Two more rules from the textbook

1. Always capitalise: Polish, German, Spanish — every nationality word starts with a capital letter.
2. To talk about the nation as a whole group, use the + adjective/nation: the Poles, the Spanish, the Dutch. Do NOT say «Polish people» — say «the Poles».

🎯 Drill after Pattern C · 3 questions

C1Anna is ___ from Warsaw.
One person → a Pole. NOT «a Polish» (that's only an adjective, e.g. «a Polish city»).
C2Pablo is ___ from Madrid.
One person → a Spaniard. NOT «a Spanish» (adjective only).
C3Many ___ live in Berlin now.
Many people → Turks (with -s, Pattern C special word).

📖 How to describe a flag

Use the pattern: The flag of X is + colours + pattern. Common patterns: three horizontal stripes · three vertical stripes · a cross · a circle · a star · a crescent.
🇮🇹
The flag of Italy has three vertical stripes: green, white and red. The green is on the LEFT. Same colours as Mexico — but no eagle!
🇲🇽
Mexico has three vertical stripes — green, white, red — with an eagle holding a snake in the middle. That is the difference from Italy.
🇩🇪
The German flag has three horizontal stripes: black, red and gold. Top to bottom.
🇪🇸
Spain has three horizontal stripes: red, yellow (bigger) and red — with a coat of arms on the left.
🇵🇹
Portugal is split into green (left, smaller) and red (right, bigger) — with a yellow shield in the middle.
🇬🇷
Greece has nine horizontal stripes — five blue and four white — and a white cross on a blue square in the top-left corner.
🇹🇷
The flag of Turkey is red, with a white crescent moon and a white star next to it.
🇵🇱
Poland has just two horizontal stripes — white on top, red on bottom. The simplest flag of your 9.
🇵🇪
Peru has three vertical stripes — red, white, red — with a small shield in the middle. Careful: Poland's stripes are HORIZONTAL, not vertical.

🎯 Now test yourself · read the clue → tap the correct flag

F1«Two horizontal stripes: white on top, red on bottom. Nothing else.»
🇵🇱 Poland — the simplest flag in our list. Peru has VERTICAL stripes, not horizontal.
F2«Three vertical stripes — green, white, red — AND an eagle with a snake in the middle.»
🇲🇽 Mexico. Italy has the SAME colours but NO eagle. That's the trick!
F3«Green on the left (smaller) and red on the right (bigger), with a yellow shield in the middle.»
🇵🇹 Portugal. The only flag in our 9 with a green-red split and a shield.
F4«Three VERTICAL stripes: red, white, red. With a small shield in the middle.»
🇵🇪 Peru. Poland has HORIZONTAL stripes and only two colours (white + red).
F5«Three horizontal stripes — red, yellow (bigger), red — with a coat of arms on the left of the yellow band.»
🇪🇸 Spain. Germany also has 3 horizontal stripes but the colours are black-red-gold.
