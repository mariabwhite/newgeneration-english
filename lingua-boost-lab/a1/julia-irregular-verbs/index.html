What is an irregular verb? 🔀

Warm-up · regular vs irregular · listen & compare

🧠 The one rule you need

To make Past Simple of a regular verb, we add -ed: play → played, cook → cooked, watch → watched.
But some verbs are old and stubborn — they change in their own way. These are irregular verbs.
There is no rule — we just learn them. Good news: they are only about 100, and today we meet all of them.

3

Meet the 4 patterns 🧩

All 100 verbs fall into these four families

🧩 Four families, one lesson

Do not try to memorise all 100 at once. Learn the pattern, then the verb sits in the right box in your head.

A · Same-same-same

cut → cut → cut
put → put → put
hit → hit → hit
let → let → let
No change at all. Only your intonation tells the tense.

B · Past = Participle

buy → bought → bought
bring → brought → brought
say → said → said
make → made → made
Base changes once, and the new form serves for both past and participle.

C · Vowel triple

sing → sang → sung
drink → drank → drunk
swim → swam → swum
begin → began → begun
i–a–u. A little song inside the verb.

D · Three different words

go → went → gone
see → saw → seen
eat → ate → eaten
take → took → taken
Three completely different shapes — the biggest family.

📌 Today we mostly work with the middle column — Past Simple. The third column (participle) we will meet again when we do Present Perfect.

Say · tell · hear · see 🗣️

Verbs of talking, listening, seeing · easy to mix up

🗣️ Do not mix them up

say / said — no person: She said "hi".
tell / told — with a person: She told me a story.
speak / spoke — about a language or a talk: I spoke in English.
hear / heard — sound arrives to your ear.
see / saw — image arrives to your eye.
feel / felt — for feelings and body sense.

The Old Bakery on Rose Street 🥐

A short story · about 500 words · violet words = irregular past

🎧 How to read

Read the whole story once, then tap the button to hear it read aloud. Tap any violet verb to hear that verb alone with its base form.
🔍 Find 15 irregular verbs · 0 / 15

Last Sunday afternoon, Julia and her friend Sonya set out on a little adventure. They had heard from Julia's grandma that the old bakery on Rose Street was closing after fifty-two years, so they decided to go and say goodbye to Aunt Marta, the baker.

The afternoon sun shone brightly, and Julia's little dog Toby led the way, wagging his tail. When the girls came around the corner, they saw a small crowd standing outside the bakery. A big paper hung in the window, and someone had written on it: "Thank you for fifty-two beautiful years."

Aunt Marta stood in the doorway. She wore her flour-white apron and held a wooden spoon. When she saw the girls, she smiled and waved. "Come in, come in! I saved a warm cinnamon bun for each of you."

Inside, the smell of fresh bread was everywhere. Julia bit into her bun and closed her eyes. Sonya ate hers slowly and said, "Aunt Marta, why are you closing?"

The old baker sat down and sighed. "I grew tired, my dear. And I lost my grandmother's secret recipe last month. Without it, my honey cake is not the same." She felt sad. "I have sought that little brown notebook for six weeks. I dug through every drawer, I swept every corner — nothing."

Julia's eyes shone. "Can we help you look?"

"You can try," Aunt Marta laughed. "But do not spread the flour on my clean floor!"

The girls searched everywhere. Sonya slid a small ladder to the top shelf and dug behind the old cake tins. Julia lay down on the wooden floor and stuck her head under the counter. Toby stole a crumb of bread and hid behind a chair.

Suddenly, Sonya cried out. "Julia, look!" Between two old cookbooks, she found a tiny brown notebook. Its cover was torn, its pages were yellow. She held it up like a trophy.

Aunt Marta wept a little. She had not seen the notebook since her grandmother had given it to her sixty years earlier. "You brought back my whole family," she whispered. "You dear little detectives!"

That afternoon, Aunt Marta taught the girls how to make honey cake the old way. Julia beat the eggs, Sonya spread the honey, and together they built a warm, golden cake. When it came out of the oven, the smell filled the whole street. People came in with wide eyes.

"Maybe I will not close after all," Aunt Marta said quietly.

By evening, the sun had set and the streetlamps had lit up. Julia and Sonya walked home tired but proud. They swung their empty bag between them and sang a silly song. Julia thought about the day. She had spent only three hours at the bakery, but she felt she had lived a whole little story.

At home, she sat down and wrote in her diary: "Today we found something old, and something old found us back."

— The end —

Second story · The Secret Key 🗝️

Another A2+ story · adventure · Julia, Sonya and the hidden door

🎧 How to read

Same as before: read the whole story once, then tap to hear it read aloud. Tap any violet verb for base → past.
🔍 Find 15 irregular verbs · 0 / 15

One warm afternoon last summer, Julia and Sonya went exploring behind the old town library. Nobody had told them what lay there — and that made it even more exciting.

Between two thick ivy vines, Julia's eyes shone. She saw a loose red brick in the wall. She pulled it — carefully — and it slid out into her hand. A cool wind came through the little hole. Then, with a soft creak, a small wooden door swung open on its own.

Sonya's mouth fell open. "It was hidden all this time," she whispered. Toby, their little dog, hid behind Julia's leg for a moment, then bravely led the way.

Inside was a small forgotten room. Dust lay thick on the shelves. Old cobwebs hung from the ceiling like grey lace. A pale beam of sun shone through a narrow window and lit up a wooden trunk in the corner.

Julia sat down beside the trunk. Someone had swept a small circle of dust away from the top, as if a person had been here not long ago. Sonya's heart beat fast. On top of the trunk, a small brown key stuck up from a slit in the wood.

Julia held the key gently. She turned it, and the lock clicked open. Inside they found a small diary and a little velvet bag. Sonya opened the bag and gasped — inside were three old copper coins and a folded map.

Julia read the first page of the diary aloud:
"If you found this, you followed the path we made. Take one coin, take the map. The last treasure is yours."

The girls looked at each other. They chose one coin each, laid the third one gently back, and took the map. Then they wrote a small note on the last empty page of the diary: "Thank you. We will keep the story safe. — Julia and Sonya."

Toby stuck his nose into the bag, then sat proudly like a tiny guard. Julia laid a hand on his soft head. Sonya put the diary back into the trunk, and together they slid it into the corner where they had found it.

Before they left, they stood in the doorway for a long moment. The little room felt warm now, as if it had waited a very long time to be found. Sonya lit an old candle on the shelf — just for a second — and its light danced across the walls. Julia thought about the person who had built this hidden room. Whoever they were, they had left something kind.

The girls stepped back into the sunlight. They laughed, they held their coins tight, and they knew that this little secret would stay with them forever.

— The end —

Bonus story · The Little Shelter 🐾

A very warm short story · quiet kindness · about 450 words · no quiz
💛 Bonus · gentle read
The Little Shelter
Sunny Hill · quiet love

💛 Just for reading

No quiz, no gaps — only warmth. Notice all the small ways Julia and Sonya help animals who need quiet love.
Bonus story · listening
Press play to hear it read aloud (about 3 minutes)

Last Saturday morning, Julia and Sonya woke up early because they had a special plan. Julia's grandma had told them about a small animal shelter on Sunny Hill, and the girls wanted to help. They put on their warmest jumpers, took a bag of old blankets and some cat treats, and walked up the hill together.

When they came in, a kind volunteer named Anna gave each of them a soft green apron. "You have to be very gentle here," she said. "Some of our animals are very old, and some are getting better slowly. What they need most is quiet love."

The first room was full of cats. In a warm corner, on an old red pillow, lay a small white cat named Snowball. Her eyes were half-closed and her breathing was slow. "This is Snowball," Anna said softly. "She is a little sleepy today, but every week she gets a bit stronger. She just needs friends."

Julia sat down next to the pillow. She spoke in a very quiet voice: "Hi, Snowball. I brought you a soft blanket." Slowly, very slowly, Snowball opened one eye. Then the other. Julia laid the blanket beside her and gently stroked her tiny head. Snowball began to purr. Anna smiled and whispered, "She hasn't purred in three days. You brought that sound back."

Sonya went to the dogs' room. A very shy little dog named Biscuit had hidden behind a chair for weeks. Sonya sat on the floor and did not move. She sang a very quiet song. After ten long minutes, Biscuit came out slowly. He sniffed her hand and lay down beside her. His tail swung just a little. Anna said, "That is the first time he has come close to a person since he arrived here."

The girls fed Snowball with a tiny spoon. They cleaned an old bowl. They swept the floor. They wrote little name cards for each cat and dog. Toby, who had come with them, kept quiet watch by the door, like a small brown guard.

Before they left, Anna gave them each a small photo of Snowball and Biscuit. "You gave them a very good day," she said. "And they gave you something too, I think."

On the way home, Julia and Sonya did not speak much. They just held their photos tight. Julia thought about how a soft blanket, a quiet song and a tiny bit of warm love can help someone who is hurting. She felt full and calm and a little bit brave.

That evening, Julia sat on her bed with her own little cat curled up next to her. She stroked her softly and said, "Thank you for staying with me."

— Sometimes the smallest act of love is the biggest medicine. —
