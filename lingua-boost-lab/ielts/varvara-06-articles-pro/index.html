M1

Module 1 · Generic ONE · six impersonal registers ranked

L5 opened the door; here we walk in. Six ways to escape generic YOU — ranked by formality, with CAE keyword transformations at the end.

📖 Reference · six impersonal registers, formal → casual

1 · ONE + singular verb (C2, essays, editorials): One is often asked whether… Pair with one's / oneself. In BrE academic style, one is not stiff; in AmE it can sound aloof.
2 · Impersonal PASSIVE (safe for reports & Task 1/2): It is often argued that… · Such claims are widely rejected. No named agent.
3 · IT-CLEFT / EMPTY-IT + adjective + that-clause: It is essential that every candidate submit two references. — subjunctive base verb after essential/vital/crucial.
4 · NOMINALISATION (verb → noun subject): The rise of streaming has reshaped viewing habits. Instead of People stream more now, so they watch differently.
5 · PEOPLE / INDIVIDUALS / READERS / USERS (mid-neutral): Individuals rarely check their sources. — good for body paragraphs.
6 · WE / OUR SOCIETY (inclusive, semi-formal): fine for Part 3 Speaking, Editorial. Avoid in academic essays.
Trap: one must stay one — never mix with he/she/you/they. One should back up their files → One should back up one's files (BrE) or People should back up their files (fix by dropping one).

M2

Module 2 · Articles with names of people · the Browns, a Rembrandt, Alexander the Great

A whole zone the standard textbook skips. Five patterns, each with its own drill.

📖 Reference · five patterns · when a proper name takes an article

1 · ZERO article with a personal name. I met Peter yesterday. · Sarah is my sister. The default. Applies to first names, full names, celebrities.
2 · the + plural surname = the family. The Browns are coming to dinner. · The Kennedys shaped US politics. Verb is plural — the Browns is.
3 · the + adjective + surname = a specific known person. The young Mozart astonished Vienna. · The great Rembrandt died in poverty. The adjective picks one phase or aspect of one known person.
4 · a/an + surname = one of many · a work by · an unknown member. Three sub-uses: She owns a Rembrandt (= a painting by him) · A Mrs Watson called for you (= someone I don't know called Watson) · He is a Kennedy (= one of that family).
5 · the + epithet after the name = the historical title. Alexander the Great · Ivan the Terrible · Peter the First · Catherine the Great. Note the article stays even mid-sentence — the reign of Ivan the Terrible.
Bonus · the + surname alone (dropped adjective, high-formal): the Blake exhibition · the Kennedy years. Same as pattern 3 without the adjective — the noun modifies the following one.

Module 3 · Lexical pairs · historic/historical · economic/economical · policy/politics · classic/classical

Adjectives from the same root, split meanings. In IELTS Writing a wrong choice costs 0.5 band in Lexical Resource.

📖 Reference · the four pairs at a glance

RootA · sense 1B · sense 2
HISTORYhistoric = famous / important in history: a historic speech · a historic victoryhistorical = connected with history / real in history: historical novel · historical evidence · a historical figure
ECONOMYeconomic = related to the economy: economic growth · economic policy · economic crisiseconomical = saving money / efficient: an economical car · an economical solution
POLITICSpolicy [noun, C] = a course of action: a foreign policy · school policy on phonespolitics [noun, U + sing verb] = the activity of governing: Politics is important.
CLASSclassic = definitive of its kind: a classic mistake · a classic novel (best-of-genre)classical = of Greek/Roman antiquity OR of Western art music: classical Rome · classical music · classical languages
Article rule tucked inside: a historic X uses a, not an — the /h/ is pronounced in modern BrE. Old writers say an historic; today a historic is standard.
Bonus pair · sensible vs sensitive: sensible = reasonable, showing good judgment; sensitive = easily hurt / reacting to small changes. She's a sensitive woman with good judgment → She's a sensible woman…

M4

Module 4 · Dual-nature nouns · chicken / a chicken · work / a work · iron / an iron

The zone L5 pointed at from the door. Same noun, two senses, two article systems. Get one wrong — meaning shifts.

📖 Reference · five dual-nature patterns

1 · Animal → meat / plant → substance. A chicken ran across the road (bird) · chicken for lunch (meat). Same for fish, lamb, duck, turkey. Fruit: an apple (fruit) · apple pie (fruit as flavour). Drinks: coffee / a coffee (a cup of).
2 · Abstract → concrete instance. experience (U, expertise) · an experience (C, event). Work (U, activity) · a work / works (C, artistic pieces). Room (U, space) · a room (C, part of a building). Time (U) · a time (C, occasion). Language (U, faculty) · a language (C, English/Russian).
3 · Material → object made of it. glass (U) · a glass (C, vessel). Paper (U) · a paper (C, newspaper/essay). Iron (U, metal) · an iron (C, device). Hair (U, mass) · a hair (C, single strand). Stone (U) · a stone (C, one piece).
4 · Uncountable → served in a unit. a piece of advice · a piece of information · a slice of bread · a sheet of paper · an item of luggage · a bar of chocolate · a bottle of wine · a cup of tea · a grain of rice · a puff of smoke · a bolt of lightning.
5 · Some nouns are countable only when they name a kind of the thing. cheese (U) · a delicious French cheese (C, a variety). beer (U) · a Belgian beer (C, a type). Same shape: wine, tea, soap, shampoo, oil.

M5

Module 5 · Verb agreement · singular-only, plural-only, same-form nouns

"The news are…", "Mathematics are…", "The team are/is…" — this zone eats bands off Task 1 charts and every letter.

📖 Reference · four categories

1 · Always SINGULAR verb (-s ending, but treated as U): news, mathematics, physics, economics, politics, athletics, ethics (subject), statistics (subject), gymnastics, linguistics, phonetics, aerobics, measles, mumps, diabetes. The news is bad. · Mathematics is difficult.
Also singular: a series, a species, a means — same singular form even though ending in -s. A new means of transport is needed.
2 · Always PLURAL verb: scissors, jeans, trousers, glasses, pyjamas, tongs, tweezers, binoculars, headphones, goods, clothes, arms, riches, savings, thanks, outskirts, surroundings, remains, contents, stairs. Where are my scissors? · The goods have arrived.
People-collective, always plural verb: the police, cattle, poultry, people (= persons). The police is investigating → The police are investigating.
3 · Same singular AND plural form: sheep, deer, fish, salmon, trout, aircraft, spacecraft, series, species, means, headquarters. Verb agrees with sense: one sheep is · three sheep are.
4 · COLLECTIVE nouns · split BrE. team, family, government, staff, committee, audience, jury, crew, class, company, band, choir. Singular when the group acts as one unit; plural when members act separately. The team is winning (as a whole). · The team are arguing among themselves (individuals). AmE prefers singular by default; BrE splits freely.
Special case · headquarters: both singular and plural verb are accepted. The company's headquarters is / are in Zurich.

M6

Module 6 · Prepositions with ABOUT · idioms · articles with abstract nouns

A tight closing module: the productive verb + about family, the be about to pattern, and the rule for articles with abstract nouns like love, peace, happiness.

📖 Reference · six ABOUT patterns + abstract article rule

1 · Feeling + about: worried / anxious / nervous / excited / enthusiastic / crazy / mad / passionate + about + noun / -ing.
2 · Verb + about (cognition): know / hear / think / read / write / talk / speak / complain / joke + about. Not discuss about → discuss (transitive).
3 · come about = to happen (usually unexpectedly). How did this misunderstanding come about?
4 · be about to + infinitive = on the point of doing (very near future). I was about to leave when the phone rang. Cannot use with a time expression (not tomorrow).
5 · about as ≈ "approximately" (with numbers): about ten people · about half.
6 · What about + -ing? · How about + -ing? = suggestions. How about meeting on Thursday?
Abstract-article rule · zero article for the general concept. Peace is a right, not a privilege. · Happiness cannot be bought. · Love conquers all. Add the only when the abstract noun is post-modified: the happiness of a quiet morning · the peace of the countryside.
Add a/an only when the abstract noun is pre-modified with an adjective giving a specific type / degree: a deep sorrow · a strange happiness · a rare kindness.
