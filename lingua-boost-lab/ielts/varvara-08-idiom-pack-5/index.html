a bookworm
/ˈbʊk.wɜːm/

/kɜːl ʌp/

/liːnz/

/liːf/

🧪L3 · Fix the mistake + open endingLevel 3

Part A · Fix the wrong idiom (type the corrected sentence)
1. She's such a bookworm — she never opens a book.

→ The idiom contradicts the context. Rewrite with the correct meaning.

💡 Model

She's not much of a bookworm — she never opens a book.

2. He turned over an old leaf and started drinking again.

→ The idiom is "new leaf", and the behaviour should be positive.

💡 Model

He turned over a new leaf and gave up drinking for good.

3. I'm doing it by the books, every single step.

→ The idiom uses singular, not plural.

💡 Model

I'm doing it by the book, every single step.

4. Her poker face makes her a complete open book.

→ Poker face and open book are opposites.

💡 Model

Her poker face is impossible to read — she's definitely not an open book.

🏗L4 · Band 7+ structures · rewrite with structure + idiomLevel 4

→ Use: bookworm + read between the lines

💡 Model

💡 Model

→ Use: read between the lines

💡 Model

7.

💡 Model

💡 Model

10.

→ Use: hit the books

💡 Model

🎤Speaking · Part 1 / Part 2 / Part 313 questions

Part 1 · warm-up

  1. How often do you read books?
  2. What kinds of books do you enjoy?
  3. Do you prefer paper books, e-books, or audiobooks?
  4. Did your parents read to you when you were little?
Source · IELTS Liz · BC Part 1 bank

Part 2 · cue-card

  1. Describe a book that influenced you. Say what it was, when you read it, and how it changed your thinking.
  2. Describe a time you judged something by its cover and later changed your mind.
  3. Describe your reading habits — when, where and why you read.
Source · Cutting Edge Advanced · IELTS Simon · Cambridge 17

Part 3 · abstract

  1. Do people read less now than they did 30 years ago? Why or why not?
  2. In the age of short-form video, is "reading between the lines" a lost skill?
  3. What role does literature play in shaping a society's values?
  4. Should schools make classic literature compulsory, or let students choose?
  5. Will printed books survive in the long run, or will they disappear?
  6. Can non-fiction books really change the way a reader thinks, or do readers only accept ideas they already agree with?
Source · IELTS Advantage · ChatGPT P3 generator · Cambridge 18 · The Guardian books

a quick fix

/ˈjuː.zə ˌfrend.li/

to be up and running
/ʌp ənd ˈrʌn.ɪŋ/

(of a system) functioning normally, usually after installation or repair
