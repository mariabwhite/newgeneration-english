01

📚 Books · readers, writers, pages

18 min

🎴Idiom cards6 phrases

a bookworm
/ˈbʊk.wɜːm/
a person who reads a lot, almost constantly
My sister's such a bookworm — she finishes a novel every week.
to curl up with a good book
/kɜːl ʌp/
to settle down cosily and enjoy a book, usually in a comfortable spot
On rainy evenings I love to curl up with a good book and a cup of tea.
to judge a book by its cover
/ˈkʌv.ə/
to form an opinion from outward appearance alone
He looks grumpy, but don't judge a book by its cover — he's actually very warm.
a page-turner
/ˈpeɪdʒ ˌtɜː.nə/
a very exciting, gripping book you can't put down
Her latest thriller is a real page-turner — I read it in one sitting.
to hit the books
/hɪt ðə bʊks/
to start studying hard, especially before an exam
I'd love to come out, but I really have to hit the books tonight.
to read between the lines
/liːnz/
to understand hidden or unspoken meaning
She didn't complain openly, but if you read between the lines, she was upset.

🎤Speaking · Level 1 / 2 / 312 questions

Level 1 · warm-up (Part 1)

  1. How often do you read books?
  2. What kinds of books do you enjoy?
  3. Do you prefer paper books, e-books, or audiobooks?
  4. Did your parents read to you when you were little?
Source · IELTS Liz · ieltsliz.com

Level 2 · personal + opinion (Part 2)

  1. Describe a book that influenced you. Say what it was, when you read it, and how it changed your thinking.
  2. Talk about a time you judged something by its cover and later changed your mind.
  3. Describe your reading habits — when, where and why you read.
Source · Cutting Edge Advanced · IELTS Simon

Level 3 · abstract (Part 3)

  1. Do people read less now than they did 30 years ago? Why or why not?
  2. In the age of short-form video, is reading between the lines a lost skill?
  3. What role does literature play in shaping a society's values?
  4. Will printed books survive in the long run, or will they disappear?
Source · IELTS Advantage · ChatGPT P3 generator · Cambridge 18
🎧 mini-dialogue · idiom-rich
A: Fancy coming out tonight? B: Can't — I'm hitting the books before Friday's exam. A: You're such a bookworm! B: I know, but this one is a real page-turner, so I don't even mind.

💻 Technology · devices, AI, the digital life

18 min

🎴Idiom cards6 phrases

cutting-edge
/ˈkʌt.ɪŋ edʒ/
the most advanced, newest kind of technology
The lab uses cutting-edge AI models to analyse medical scans.
a double-edged sword
/ˈdʌb.əl edʒd sɔːd/
something with both clear benefits and clear drawbacks
Social media is a double-edged sword — it connects us but also isolates us.
behind the times
/bɪˈhaɪnd/
old-fashioned, not up to date
My uncle still uses a flip phone — he's completely behind the times.
a digital native
/ˈdɪdʒ.ɪ.təl ˈneɪ.tɪv/
a person who grew up with digital technology
My ten-year-old is a digital native; she navigates apps instinctively.
information overload
/ˌəʊ.vəˈləʊd/
too much information to process at once
Checking three news apps before breakfast gives me information overload.
to pull the plug (on sth)
/pʊl ðə plʌɡ/
to stop or cancel a project / service
The company pulled the plug on the beta app after poor sales.

🎤Speaking · Level 1 / 2 / 312 questions

Level 1 · warm-up (Part 1)

  1. How many hours a day do you spend on your phone?
  2. What apps do you use most?
  3. Are you good at learning new technology?
  4. Did your parents use computers when you were a child?
Source · IELTS Liz · Preply blog

Level 2 · personal + opinion (Part 2)

  1. Describe a piece of technology you can't live without. Say what it is, how often you use it, and why it matters to you.
  2. Talk about a time technology failed you — what happened and how you solved it.
  3. Describe an app or website that changed the way you do something.
Source · Cambridge 17 · Cutting Edge Advanced

Level 3 · abstract (Part 3)

  1. Is AI more of a help or a threat to ordinary workers?
  2. Should children under 12 have smartphones?
  3. In what ways is social media a double-edged sword?
  4. Will physical offices still exist in twenty years, or will remote tech kill them off?
Source · IELTS Advantage · Claude P3 generator · The Guardian
🎧 mini-dialogue · idiom-rich

🏛 Museums + Art · galleries, exhibitions, taste

18 min

🎴Idiom cards6 phrases

a hidden gem
/ˈhɪd.ən dʒem/
a wonderful place few people know about
The little museum behind the cathedral is a real hidden gem.
a feast for the eyes
/fiːst/
something visually stunning, a pleasure to look at
The Monet room was an absolute feast for the eyes.
to steal the show
/stiːl/
to attract the most attention; to be the most impressive
A small bronze sculpture in the corner completely stole the show.
to see sth in a new light
/laɪt/
to understand something from a fresh perspective
After the guided tour I saw Picasso's early work in a completely new light.
to leave sth to the imagination
/ɪˌmædʒ.ɪˈneɪ.ʃən/
to not show or explain everything, letting the viewer fill it in
The sculptor's half-finished figures deliberately leave a lot to the imagination.
off the beaten track
/ˈbiː.tən træk/
not well-known or touristy; unusual and quiet
We stumbled across a tiny gallery completely off the beaten track.

🚲 Bikes + Transport · getting around, journeys

18 min

🎴Idiom cards6 phrases

to get back in the saddle
/ˈsæd.əl/
to return to an activity after a break
After six months off, it felt great to get back in the saddle at work.
it's like riding a bike
/ˈraɪ.dɪŋ ə baɪk/
a skill you never forget, even after a long break
I hadn't skied for ten years, but it's like riding a bike — it came back in minutes.
(life) in the fast lane
/fɑːst leɪn/
an exciting, busy, often stressful lifestyle
After five years of life in the fast lane in London, she moved to a small village.
to miss the boat
/mɪs ðə bəʊt/
to miss an opportunity by acting too late
I thought about applying for the grant, but I totally missed the boat.
to hit the road
/hɪt ðə rəʊd/
to leave; to start a journey
It's getting late — we really should hit the road.
to go the extra mile
/ˈek.strə maɪl/
to make a special effort beyond what is required
Our taxi driver went the extra mile and helped us carry all the bags upstairs.

🏠 Home + Hometown · where you live and belong

18 min

🎴Idiom cards6 phrases

home sweet home
/swiːt həʊm/
a warm expression for the comfort of being home
After three weeks on the road, walking through the door felt like home sweet home.
to feel at home
/fiːl/
to feel comfortable and relaxed somewhere
I only lived in Lisbon for a year, but I felt completely at home there.
a stone's throw away
/stəʊnz θrəʊ/
very close by, within a very short distance
Our flat is a stone's throw away from the river.
to put down roots
/ruːts/
to settle in a particular place long-term
After years of moving around, they finally put down roots in Porto.
there's no place like home
/pleɪs/
nothing else can match the feeling of being home
I love travelling, but there's no place like home.
a creature of habit
/ˈkriː.tʃə əv ˈhæb.ɪt/
a person who follows a strict daily routine
I'm a creature of habit — same café, same seat, same coffee every morning.
