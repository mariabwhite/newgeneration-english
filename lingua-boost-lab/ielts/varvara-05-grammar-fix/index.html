M1

Module 1 · Articles · missing + comparison-shift

"helpful device" · "computer at home" · "on the laptop... than on your phone"

📖 Theory · five rules you are breaking

1 · Countable singular ALWAYS carries an article. helpful device → a helpful device. A bare singular noun does not exist (except proper names, months, mass nouns).
2 · First mention = a/an; second mention = the. I bought a laptop. The laptop broke a week later.
3 · Symmetry in comparisons. If the left side uses the, the right side must match. on the laptop... than on your phone → on a laptop... than on a phone (generic) or on the laptop... than on the phone (specific).
4 · The with superlatives, unique objects, ordinals: the best, the first, the internet, the environment.
5 · Zero article for abstract general concepts: Life is short. Money doesn't buy happiness.

M2

Module 2 · Uncountable nouns · advice, information, research

"advices" ×2 · "quite an interesting advice" — this same root damages your Writing band

📖 Theory · 15 nouns you may be handling wrongly

Uncountable → no plural, no a/an. Never: an advice · advices · a research · researches · an information.
To count units: a piece of advice · pieces of information · items of luggage · articles of clothing · sheets of paper · slices of bread.
Quantifiers: much · little · a little with uncountable · many · few · a few with countable.
Danger list: advice · information · research · knowledge · furniture · luggage · equipment · homework · progress · news · money · traffic · weather · accommodation · staff · feedback · vocabulary.
Two-faced trap: experience — a good experience (an event) vs years of experience (uncountable = expertise).

M3

Module 3 · Simple vs Continuous · when Present Continuous is wrong

"we are relying too much" · "we are always waiting" — these -ing forms should be Simple

📖 Theory · four markers that block -ing

1 · State verbs (rely, know, believe, understand, need, want, love, hate, own, belong, contain, seem, mean, prefer) — we are relying → we rely.
2 · Repeated action / habit — always, usually, every day → Simple. we are always waiting → we always wait. Exception: always + PresCont = irritation — You're always losing your keys.
3 · General truth / fact → Simple. Water boils at 100°C. The internet changes fast.
4 · Continuous is only for: right now (I'm typing) · temporary period (I'm reading Orwell this month) · developing trend (The climate is changing) · fixed future plan (I'm meeting her tomorrow).

M4

Module 4 · Reported speech · tense harmony

"you knew that people knew that it takes time" — three different tenses, two of them wrong

📖 Theory · backshift + when NOT to backshift

Backshift when the reporting verb is past: said, told, thought, knew, realized, mentioned.
Present Simple → Past Simple · Present Cont → Past Cont · Present Perfect → Past Perfect · Past Simple → Past Perfect · will → would · can → could · may → might.
Your case: you knew that people knew that it takes time → pick one axis. All past: you knew that people understood that it took time. Or all present (universal truth): you know that people understand it takes time.
Backshift optional when the reported fact is still true or a general truth: She said the sun rises in the east. In IELTS Writing, backshift is safer whenever the reporting verb is past.
Pronouns & time shift: today → that day · tomorrow → the next day · here → there · this → that.

M5

Module 5 · Generic YOU → alternatives for IELTS Writing

"you did not expect your friend..." · "you don't usually trust random people" — in IELTS Writing generic you costs 0.5 band in Grammar

📖 Theory · five replacements (by rising formality)

1 · one (formal, C1+): One does not expect an instant reply.
2 · people / most people / individuals (neutral): People did not expect an instant reply.
3 · we / our society / users / readers (inclusive): We tend to expect instant replies now.
4 · passive (impersonal, IELTS-safe): An instant reply was not expected.
5 · general noun as subject: Users of social media rely on constant contact.
Speaking exception: generic you is fine in casual speaking (Part 1). Even there, 3-4 times per monologue is the ceiling.

M6

Module 6 · Band 8+ constructions · inversion, cleft, subjunctive, formal conditionals

The moves that push a Speaking / Writing answer from Band 7 to Band 8+. Aim for at least one per essay paragraph and one per Part-3 turn.

📖 Theory · six band-8+ moves

1 · Formal conditionals · Type 1 Should + subject + base verb. If you have any questions → Should you have any questions, please contact the office.
2 · Formal conditionals · Type 2 Were + subject + to + infinitive. If the university reduced fees → Were the university to reduce fees, more students would apply.
3 · Subjunctive after essential/vital/crucial/recommend/suggest/insist: that + subject + base verb (no -s, no should needed). It is essential that every candidate submit two references.
4 · Inversion after fronted negative adverbs: Rarely / Never / Not only / No sooner / Seldom / Hardly / Little + aux + subject. I have never seen such a clear structure → Never have I seen such a clear structure.
5 · Cleft sentences · to emphasise: What + subject + verb + is … and It is/was + X + that/who …. What we need is time. · It was the tutor who spotted the mistake.
6 · "Suggest" without to-infinitive: suggest + that + subject + (should) + base verb. Never: I suggest you to see a doctor → I suggest (that) you see a doctor.
