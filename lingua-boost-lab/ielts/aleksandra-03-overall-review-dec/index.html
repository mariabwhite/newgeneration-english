Band 7+ structures · 10 patterns you've been using

These are the ten upgrade structures from L5 · 15.10 (Idiom Pack). Every Band 7+ Speaking or Writing answer should contain at least two of them.

PatternFormulaExample
CleftIt is / was X that / who ...It is the fear of judgement that stops most people reading in public.
Pseudo-cleftWhat ... is / was ...What I value most in a book is a page-turner plot.
Inversion · Not onlyNot only ... but also ...Not only did she finish it in a day, but she also recommended it to everyone.
Inversion · Rarely / Seldom / Hardly everRarely do I ...Rarely do I come across a book that resonates with me so deeply.
Passive reportIt is said / believed / reported that ...It is believed that reading before bed improves sleep quality.
Gerund as subject-ing + is / makes ...Reading in a second language builds confidence faster than classroom work.
Participle clauseHaving + V3, S + V ...Having grown up in a bookish family, I took reading for granted.
Reduced relativea book written by ... / people living in ...A book written in simple language can still change your life.
Mixed conditionalIf I had ..., I would be ...If I had read more as a child, I would probably be a different person now.
No sooner / HardlyNo sooner ... than ... / Hardly ... when ...No sooner had I opened the book than the phone rang.
Rule: every Speaking Part 2 and every Writing T2 should contain at least two of these. In the mock at §12 we count them live.
Drill 01b · name the structure · 10 items

For each Band 7+ sentence, choose which of the 10 patterns is being used.

  1. «What I enjoy most about reading is the quiet.» →
  2. «Having lived in St Petersburg for five years, I know the museum district well.» →
  3. «It is the sound design, not the acting, that makes the film work.» →
  4. «Rarely do I finish a non-fiction book in one sitting.» →
  5. «If I hadn't moved to a bigger city, I probably wouldn't be working in this field now.» →
  6. «No sooner had the trailer appeared than the film became a global hit.» →
  7. «Living alone in a new city taught me more than any book ever has.» →
  8. «It is widely believed that bilingual children develop empathy earlier.» →
  9. «A film shot entirely in black and white can still feel completely modern.» →
  10. «Not only did it win the award, but it also broke the box-office record.» →
Answer key
  1. pseudo-cleft — «What I ... is ...»
  2. participle clause — «Having lived ...»
  3. cleft — «It is the sound design ... that makes ...»
  4. inversion (Rarely) — verb comes before subject after «Rarely»
  5. mixed conditional — if-clause past perfect + main clause present cond
  6. no sooner ... than — two linked events
  7. gerund subject — «Living alone taught ...»
  8. passive report — «It is believed that ...»
  9. reduced relative — «[which was] shot entirely in b/w»
  10. inversion (Not only ... but also) — auxiliary before subject
01c

Articles Pro · 4 modules recap

Compressed recap of the four modules from L2 · 06.10. These are the four Articles / Uncountables areas examiners target most in Speaking Part 3 and Writing T2.

Module 1 · Generic ONE · 6 impersonal registers

When you talk in general about a group, the examiner wants to hear register-sensitive impersonal forms. These are ranked from most casual to most formal:

RegisterFormExample
Casual spokenyou + verbYou never really switch off completely.
Neutral writtenpeople + verbPeople increasingly value time away from screens.
Semi-formalwe as a societyWe as a society are losing the ability to concentrate.
Formal writtenone + verbOne must question whether constant stimulation is healthy.
Academicit is + Vpp + that ...It is often argued that mindfulness is a middle-class luxury.
Academic passivethere is a tendency for ... to ...There is a tendency for city-dwellers to equate busyness with status.

Module 2 · Articles with names of people · 5 patterns

the Browns
plural family name with THE
The Browns live two doors down.
a Rembrandt
a work by that artist (A/AN)
She bought a Rembrandt at auction.
Alexander the Great
historical epithet uses THE
Alexander the Great founded the city of Alexandria.
a certain Mr Smith
unknown person (A/AN + full name)
A certain Mr Smith called while you were out.
the Shakespeare of our time
comparison / epithet (THE)
She's been called the Shakespeare of her generation.

Module 3 · Lexical pairs · the 4 pairs IELTS examiners test

historicvshistorical
important moment · vs · relating to history
A historic speech / a historical novel.
economicvseconomical
to do with the economy · vs · cost-saving
The economic climate / an economical car.
policyvspolitics
specific course of action · vs · the practice of governing
Immigration policy / European politics.
classicvsclassical
definitive, excellent · vs · relating to ancient Greece/Rome or 18-19c music
A classic novel / classical music.

Module 4 · Dual-nature nouns · the countable / uncountable flip

chickenvsa chicken
meat (U) · vs · the bird (C)
I don't eat chicken / A chicken crossed the road.
workvsa work
labour (U) · vs · a work of art (C)
I have a lot of work / This is a work by Rodin.
ironvsan iron
metal (U) · vs · appliance (C)
Iron rusts / I need an iron for my shirt.
papervsa paper
material (U) · vs · essay/newspaper (C)
Give me some paper / She wrote a paper on Dickens.
glassvsa glass
material (U) · vs · drinking vessel (C)
The table is made of glass / Pour me a glass of water.
hairvsa hair
on the head (U) · vs · single strand (C)
Her hair is long / There's a hair in my soup.

🎙 IELTS Part 1 · warm-up~30 sec per Q · 4 Qs
Let's talk about activities and happiness.
  1. What do you usually do to relax after a long day at work?
  2. Are you more of a homebody or the outdoorsy type?
  3. Is there an activity that always lifts your spirits, no matter what?
  4. How important is having free-time activities for your general happiness?
🎙 IELTS Part 2 · long turn1 min prep + 2 min talk
Describe an activity that makes you happy.
You should say:
  • what the activity is and when you do it;
  • whether you do it alone or with others;
  • why it has that effect on you;
and explain whether you think everyone needs an activity like this.

Band 9 model · ~2 minutes · click to reveal

«I'd like to talk about something very simple that genuinely makes me happy — baking sourdough bread on a Sunday morning. It started as a lockdown experiment and somehow stuck. I always do it alone, early, before the flat wakes up, with the kitchen window open and some quiet music in the background. What's funny is that it is the rhythm of the process — mixing, folding, waiting — that puts me in a kind of meditative state. I'm not an outdoorsy type; I'm a bit of a homebody, and baking is where I really am in my element. It's also my main way to unwind and to blow off steam after a stressful week. As for whether everyone needs an activity like this — I think so, yes. Having said that, I don't believe it has to be creative or outdoorsy; the specific activity doesn't matter. What matters is having something reliably yours, something the working week can't take from you. Without it, people risk being in a constant low-level rut.»

Band 9 moves used in this model
  • Cleft: it is the rhythm of the process that puts me in...
  • Pseudo-cleft: What matters is having something reliably yours.
  • 6 theme idioms, all used naturally: unwind · outdoorsy · homebody · in my element · blow off steam · in a rut
  • Hedging: Having said that... I don't believe it has to be...
🎙 IELTS Part 3 · discussion~45 sec per Q · 3 Qs
Let's broaden the topic: activities, happiness and modern life.
  1. Why do you think so many people struggle to find activities that genuinely make them happy nowadays?
  2. Do you think the kinds of activities that make people happy are changing between generations?
  3. Is it possible for a government to actually improve people's happiness through policy, or is it purely a personal matter?

🎙 IELTS Part 1 · warm-up~30 sec per Q · 4 Qs
Let's talk about health and daily habits.
  1. How important is physical exercise in your week?
  2. Do you think people in your country take their mental health seriously enough?
  3. What's one habit you've tried to break because it was affecting your wellbeing?
  4. Do you use any apps or devices to track your health?
🎙 IELTS Part 2 · long turn1 min prep + 2 min talk
Describe a person you know who did something to improve their health or fitness.
You should say:
  • who the person is and what they did;
  • why they decided to make this change;
  • how it affected their life;
and explain what you learned from their experience.

Band 9 model · ~2 minutes · click to reveal

«I'd like to talk about my older brother. For most of his twenties, he worked long hours in finance and essentially lived on coffee and takeaway. Then two years ago his doctor flagged early signs of hypertension — it was quite literally a wake-up call. What he did next surprised me: he didn't go for some extreme diet. Instead, he made three very boring changes — walking at least ten thousand steps a day, cooking twice a week, and putting his phone out of reach by ten in the evening to stop himself doomscrolling. The effect, over eighteen months, was striking. He lost about twelve kilos, his blood pressure normalised, and — the part I didn't expect — he became noticeably calmer. His job hadn't changed; his relationship with his job had. The lesson, for me, was less about fitness and more about this: mental health is the elephant in the room in most offices like his, and companies that pay lip service to wellbeing without changing the actual hours are a double-edged sword for their staff. What ultimately helped him wasn't willpower — it was small, visible, boring changes.»

Band 9 moves used
  • Pseudo-cleft: What he did next surprised me / What ultimately helped him wasn't willpower
  • Participle clause: putting his phone out of reach by ten in the evening to stop himself...
  • All 6 theme idioms used in context
  • Hedging: quite literally... noticeably calmer
🎙 IELTS Part 3 · discussion~45 sec per Q · 3 Qs
Let's broaden the topic: society and wellbeing.
  1. Why do you think mental health became so much more openly discussed in the last ten years?
  2. Do social media platforms have a responsibility to protect users from the comparison trap and doomscrolling?
  3. In your view, who is more responsible for employees' wellbeing — the employer or the employee themselves?

🎙 IELTS Part 1 · warm-up~30 sec per Q · 4 Qs
Let's talk about the environment.
  1. Do you do anything in your daily life to reduce your carbon footprint?
  2. What's the biggest environmental problem in your city?
  3. How concerned are people around you about climate change?
  4. Do you recycle at home? Is it easy to do where you live?
🎙 IELTS Part 2 · long turn1 min prep + 2 min talk
Describe a law to help the environment which you think your country should have.
You should say:
  • what the law would be;
  • who it would affect and how;
  • how it would work in practice;
and explain why you think this law is more important than other possible measures.

Band 9 model · ~2 minutes · click to reveal

«I'd like to propose a law that already works well in parts of Northern Europe: a legally binding polluter-pays principle for single-use packaging. In plain terms, any company that puts plastic or non-recyclable packaging onto the market would be required to pay the full cost of collecting and processing that waste — not the municipality, not the tax-payer. It would affect retailers, FMCG manufacturers and delivery platforms most of all, because these are the sectors where greenwashing is currently most obvious. In practice, the costs would flow through to prices, which would quietly push consumers towards lower-footprint options — but without the finger-wagging. Why this rather than something else? Because most environmental grassroots movements I've seen hit the same ceiling: individual action matters, but it's dwarfed by the industrial scale of the problem. Only structural levers — taxes, bans, phasing-out timetables — shift the whole system. Had this kind of law been in place ten years ago, I believe we would already be close to reaching net zero on domestic packaging. Finger-wagging, in my view, is just more of what we already have too much of.»

Band 9 moves used
  • Mixed conditional (inverted): Had this kind of law been in place ten years ago, I believe we would already be close to...
  • Passive report: any company that puts... would be required to pay...
  • All 6 theme idioms used naturally
  • Connector stack: In plain terms... In practice... Why this rather than... Only structural levers...
🎙 IELTS Part 3 · discussion~45 sec per Q · 3 Qs
Let's broaden the topic: climate, policy and individual action.
  1. Who should bear the main cost of fighting climate change — governments, companies, or individuals?
  2. Is a carbon tax a fair way to change people's behaviour, or does it punish those with lower incomes most?
  3. In your view, are grassroots movements more effective than top-down government action, or vice versa?

Writing Task 2 · 5 essay types matrix

From L7 Oct · 07.10 and L5 Nov · 24.11. These are the five essay types examiners cycle through — each with its own skeleton and Band 8 openers.

① Opinion
«To what extent do you agree or disagree?»

Take one clear side. Avoid sitting on the fence.

Intro (paraphrase + stance)
Body 1 (reason A + evidence)
Body 2 (reason B + evidence)
Conclusion (restate stance)
② Discussion
«Discuss both views and give your opinion.»

Must present both sides fairly before your own opinion.

Intro (both sides + stance)
Body 1 (view A + why people think so)
Body 2 (view B + why people think so)
Conclusion (your side + why)
③ Problem–Solution
«What are the problems? What are the solutions?»

Match each problem to a realistic solution. No fantasy fixes.

Intro (frame the issue)
Body 1 (main problems)
Body 2 (solutions — linked to the problems)
Conclusion (take-away)
④ Advantages / Disadvantages
«Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?»

The second half of the question is the decision — don't skip it.

Intro (paraphrase + stance)
Body 1 (advantages)
Body 2 (disadvantages)
Conclusion (overall judgement)
⑤ Two-part question
Two direct questions (Q1 + Q2)

Answer both — one body per question.

Intro (paraphrase both Qs)
Body 1 (answer to Q1)
Body 2 (answer to Q2)
Conclusion (short summary of both)

Band 8 upgrade phrases · by position

  • Opener: In my view · It is widely argued that · This essay will explore ...
  • Body connector: The primary reason ... · A second consideration ... · This can be attributed to ... · Proponents argue that ... · Detractors claim that ... · In the long run ...
  • Hedging: arguably · bearing in mind · to a large extent · tangible benefits · a double-edged sword
  • Pivot: Having said that ... · That said ... · Notwithstanding ...
  • Closer: To round off, the evidence suggests that ... · On balance, I would argue that ...

Tactics · per task type

16.2 · error correction · 8 Band 7+ attempts
