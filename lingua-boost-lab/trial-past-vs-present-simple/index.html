🐱 Meet Emma · read this first

Twelve small games. In each game Emma the cat helps you learn one tiny thing. Ready? Let's play! 🎈

  1. Two tenses. Only two! ✨ I eat fish every day — that's Present Simple (things you always do). Yesterday I ate fish — that's Past Simple (one time in the past).
  2. Little words help you 🎯. See every, always, usually? → Present. See yesterday, last week, ago? → Past. Easy!
  3. Questions use helpers 🙋: do / does for today, did for yesterday. Say "Do you like fish?" or "Did you eat fish?"
  4. Some verbs are funny 🐛: go → went, eat → ate, see → saw. You'll meet 10 of them at Station 06.

🌞 vs 🌙 · Two tenses at a glance

🌞 Present Simple

When?

every day · usually · always · never · on Mondays

I / you / we / they

I play. You eat fish. We go to school.

he / she / it (add -s!)

He plays. She eats. Emma sleeps.

Question

Do you play? Does she eat?

No

I don't play. He doesn't eat.

🌙 Past Simple

When?

yesterday · last week · two days ago · in 2020

Same for everybody!

I played. She played. They played.

Regular verb

play → played, cook → cooked, jump → jumped

Question

Did you play? Did she eat?

No

I didn't play. He didn't eat.

✨ The big rule: did / didn't are for yesterday. After did / didn't the verb stays SHORT: Did she eat? — NOT Did she ate?

🌞 The magic S · for he / she / it

In Present Simple only he, she or it put a little -s on the verb.

  • Just add -s (most verbs) → plays, eats, runs, sleeps
  • Verbs that end in -o, -s, -sh, -ch, -x → add -es → go → goes, wash → washes, watch → watches, fix → fixes
  • Consonant + y → change y to i and add -es → study → studies, fly → flies, cry → cries
  • Special little ones → have → has, be → is, do → does
🐱 Emma plays. Emma watches birds. Emma flies to the ball. Emma has a red collar.

🌙 The little -ed at the end

To make a Past Simple regular verb, add -ed. But careful — sometimes the shape changes a little!

  • Just add -ed → play → played, cook → cooked, jump → jumped
  • Verb ends in -e → add only -d → love → loved, live → lived, smile → smiled
  • Consonant + y → change y to i and add -ed → study → studied, cry → cried
  • Short verb ends in consonant → double the last letter → stop → stopped, plan → planned, hug → hugged

🔊 How to say -ed · three little sounds

The -ed ending has three sounds — but you don't choose, the last letter of the verb chooses for you!

🎯 /t/
short "t" sound
after these letters: p · k · f · s · sh · ch · x
walked · watched · jumped · kissed · washed
🎵 /d/
soft "d" sound
after all other sounds and vowels
played · opened · lived · called · cleaned
🎤 /ɪd/
extra syllable "id"
after t or d (adds 1 syllable!)
wanted · needed · waited · ended · visited
👂 Try this trick: say the verb, then say the ending. If your tongue touches the back of your teeth twice → it's /ɪd/. If only once → it's /t/ or /d/. Tap any orange word to hear it!

🐱 Emma's Big Day

🔊 Listen first · then read
Show script 📝

Hello! My name is Emma. I am a small cat. I live with a boy — his name is Tom. Every day I eat fish for breakfast. I play with a red ball. I sleep in the sun on Tom's bed. My friend Milo is a big cat. He lives next door.
