Shopping clichés · fixed phrases you must know by heart

Every shop encounter runs on 5-second phrases. Learn these — you'll use them every time.

Rule: in a shop you almost never build a new sentence — you plug in a fixed phrase. Below are the 6 «buckets» a shopper needs. Tap any line to hear it.

👋 Walking in · assistant asks

  • Can I help you?
  • Are you looking for anything in particular?
  • Let me know if you need any help.
  • Take your time.

🙋 Your reply · what you need

  • I'm just browsing, thanks.
  • I'm looking for a warm jacket.
  • I need something for autumn.
  • Have you got anything in dark blue?
  • Do you have this in size L?

👕 Trying on

  • Can I try this on, please?
  • Where is the fitting room?
  • It doesn't fit — it's too tight / loose.
  • Have you got a bigger / smaller size?
  • This one fits perfectly.

💰 Price & discount

  • How much is it?
  • Is it on sale?
  • Is there a discount today?
  • Do you have a loyalty card?
  • Is tax included in the price?

💳 At the cash desk

  • I'll take this one, please.
  • Card or cash?
  • Do you take card?
  • Can I have a bag, please?
  • Would you like a receipt?
  • Yes, please, by email if possible.

🔁 Return · exchange

  • I'd like to return this, please.
  • Can I exchange it for a bigger size?
  • Here's the receipt.
  • Sorry, the zip is broken.
  • Would a refund or store credit be OK?
Speaking rule for today: every shop answer starts with one of these fixed phrases — never a bare «yes / no». Phrase → then your own thought.

Speak · use the phrases live

  1. You enter a shop. Assistant: «Can I help you?» — reply 3 different ways.
  2. The jacket is too tight. Ask for a bigger size — 2 different ways.
  3. You want to check the price and see if it's on sale — ask 2 questions in a row.
  4. You've decided to buy. Full checkout dialogue: 4 lines from you, from «I'll take this» to goodbye.
  5. The zip broke after one week. Come back and start the return — 3 lines minimum.
Answer formula: fixed phrase → one detail (colour / size / reason) → polite tail (please / thanks).

On Saturday morning Denis goes to the supermarket. His wife wrote a shopping list on a small piece of paper. He puts it in his jacket pocket and takes a big basket at the door.

Three City Stories · three ways of buying food

Three characters, three very different Saturday food-shopping styles. Listen · read · compare · decide whose approach is closest to yours.

Listen for: each character has a fixed phrase — Anna «I haven't been inside a supermarket in months», Sergey «I need to smell the tomato», Maya «I never shop without a list». Watch how they justify with because / for me / honestly.
A

Anna · 25 · marketing manager · Moscow

🛒 Online groceries · «my fridge is a delivery box»

«I honestly haven't been inside a supermarket for six months,» — Anna says. «On Sunday evening I open the delivery app, tap 'reorder last week', and everything comes to my door in an hour.

I buy almost the same list every time: brown bread, milk, three yoghurts, a jar of honey, a packet of cheese, six apples, one salmon fillet. Boring? Maybe. But my Saturday morning stays mine — no queue, no heavy bags, no rain.

Yes, I pay a small delivery fee. Yes, sometimes they send tomatoes that look a bit sad. But if I add up the time I save, it's the best deal of my week.»

S

Sergey · 45 · engineer · St Petersburg

🥬 Sunday market man · «I need to smell the tomato»

«I never buy food online,» — Sergey says. «I'm 45, I know my food, and I need to touch it. The tomato in the photo is always the best one — the one in the box is always the saddest.

Every Sunday at eight in the morning I walk to the farmers' market near my flat. The lady on stall seven remembers me — she saves me the best peaches. I choose every apple, smell every loaf of bread, ask about the cheese.

It takes an hour and a half. My bag is heavy on the way home. But I know exactly what I'm eating for the whole week — and that, for me, is worth the time.»

M

Maya · 32 · freelance designer · Kazan

🏷 Meal-plan warrior · «I never shop without a list»

«I never go to the shop without a list,» — Maya laughs. «Every Sunday morning I plan seven dinners for the whole week, then I write exactly what I need — nothing more, nothing less.

One trip, one basket, one receipt. Total: about three thousand five hundred roubles a week. My friends think I'm boring, but my food budget is half of theirs, and I still eat well.

My favourite trick: end-of-day discounts. Bread, yoghurt, fish — after eight in the evening the shop drops the price by fifty percent. You just need patience — and a good list.»

Compare · discussion questions

Speaking rule: start each answer with «I'm more like…», «Honestly, closer to…», «For me, it's a mix of…».

  2. Which approach do you think is cheapest in the long run? Why?
  3. Anna returns items she doesn't like. Have you ever returned something? Was it easy?
  4. Sergey says «I know my body». What piece of clothing do you always buy the same size / brand?
  5. Maya keeps a shopping list. Do you plan before you shop, or buy on impulse?
  6. If you had to teach one of them a new trick — what would you tell them?

Answer formula: pick Anna / Sergey / Maya → give one habit that matches → give one habit that doesn't.

5D · Wh-questions · price, size, colour
Rule: full form Wh-word + do/does + subject + verb. With to be: Wh-word + am/is/are + subject. Prices → How much is / are. Sizes → What size are you.
___ this jacket?
___ you?

5E · There is / are + Can I…?
Rule: There is + one thing. There are + more than one. Polite request in a shop = Can I + verb…? (Can I try, Can I pay, Can I see…).
___ a big queue at the cash desk.
___ two fitting rooms on the left.
___ this on, please?
___ by card here?
5F · This / that / these / those
Rule: this / these — near me. that / those — over there, farther. this / that — singular. these / those — plural.
Can I try ___ sneakers on, please? (holding them)
How much is ___ jacket over there?
I don't like ___ boots on the top shelf — too dark.
Look — ___ hoodie is very warm. I'll take it.
5G · Plurals (irregular) + Apostrophe 's
Rule: irregular plurals: foot → feet · child → children · person → people · woman → women · man → men. Countable «people» → many, not much. Possession 's: singular = Denis's, plural ending in -s = parents'.

Café English · Use it right

Polite ordering phrases · would like / would you like / to go / for here.

Rule: in a café you rarely build a new sentence — you plug in one of 5 phrases. «I'd like…» = polite order · «Would you like…?» = an offer · «to go» = take away · «for here» = drink in the café.

☕ Order

  • I'd like a cappuccino, please.
  • Can I have a flat white, please?
  • Could I get a small latte?

🥐 Extras

  • And a croissant, please.
  • Anything to eat with that?
  • No thanks, just the coffee.

🥛 Choices

  • Would you like milk or sugar?
  • Oat milk, please.
  • No sugar, thanks.

🥤 To go / for here

  • To go, please.
  • For here, please.
  • Can I have a lid?

💳 Pay

  • That's six pounds. Card or cash?
  • Card, please. Contactless.
  • Can I have a receipt?

Speak · order it live

  1. Order a large cappuccino to go, no sugar — 2 different ways.
  2. Ask for a croissant + say if you want a bag — full sentence.
  3. Barista: «Would you like milk or sugar?» — reply 3 different ways (yes / no / a bit of milk).
  4. Pay by card + ask for a receipt by email — full sentence.
Answer formula: polite verb (I'd like / Could I) → drink + size → to go / for here → please.

8G · Chain-questions · funnel · «Last shopping trip»

  • When did you go shopping last time?
  • Where did you go — mall, high street, or online?
  • What did you buy? Why that one?
  • How much was it? Was there a discount?
  • Have you worn / used it yet? How does it fit?
  • Will you go back to that shop? Why or why not?

Chain 2 · «My wardrobe right now»

  • How many jackets have you got in your wardrobe?
  • Which one is your favourite? What colour and material?
  • How often do you wear it?
  • What clothes do you never wear? Why do you keep them?
  • If you had 5 000 ₽ for one new item today, what would you buy?

Chain 3b · «Money & shopping»

  • Do you set a monthly budget for clothes / cafés / small pleasures?
  • Have you ever spent all your budget in the first week? How did it feel?
  • What's the smartest way you save money on everyday shopping?
  • Card or cash — which do you use more, and why?
  • If suddenly you got a 50 000 ₽ bonus tomorrow — what would you spend it on?

Chain 4 · «City weekends»

  • What's your favourite spot in your city for a weekend walk?
  • Do you prefer old town streets or new modern malls?
  • Where would you take a friend visiting from another country?
  • What's one place in your city that most tourists miss?
  • If you had to leave your city for a year, what would you miss most?

Chain 3 · «Coffee habits»

  • Do you drink coffee every day? How many cups?
  • Do you make it at home or buy at a café?
  • What's your usual order — cappuccino, latte, americano, flat white?
  • Do you take sugar or milk?
  • Which café near your home is your favourite? Why?

Exchange · big closing questions · pick 3 and go deep

Answer format: cliché → 2-3 sentences → because / for example. Minimum 45 seconds per question.
  1. What's the last thing you bought that you're really happy about? Tell the story.
  2. What's the last thing you bought and regret? Why?
  3. Describe your ideal Saturday from morning to evening — realistic, not fantasy.
  4. Which of the three characters — Anna / Sergey / Maya — would you like to teach English to? Why?
  5. If a friend from another country visited you for one Saturday, where would you take them?
  6. What's one habit around shopping / coffee / clothes you'd like to change this year?
