Greetings

3 min · 8 phrases · listen · repeat · drill
Theory

Four ways to say hello

Hi. is casual and always safe. Hello. is a touch more polite — good with strangers. Good morning / afternoon / evening is formal (before 12 / 12–18 / after 18). How are you? comes after the greeting; the standard reply is I'm fine, thanks. And you?

Two ways to introduce yourself

Where you're from

6 min · country vs city · nationalities · fix mistakes
Theory

Country, city, nationality

I'm from Russia. — country. I live in Moscow. — city, always live in. Nationality is an adjective: I'm Russian / American / Italian. Bounce back with How about you? or And you?

Common mistake: I from Moscow — missing am / 'm. The verb be is required.

What you do

7 min · jobs vocab · flashcards · builder
Theory

Three ways to answer "What do you do?"

I'm a teacher. — the short workhorse. Always with a / an. I work in marketing / in IT / in a hospital. — for a field. I work as a manager at Yandex. — role + company.

Learning? I'm a student. Retired? I'm retired. On leave? I'm on maternity leave. No boss? I'm self-employed.

Never say: I am work or I working — use I work or I'm a X.

Speaking

12 min · three different formats
Two truths & a lie

Say three sentences about yourself — two true, one false

Template: I'm from ___. I work as a ___. I live in ___. Your teacher guesses the lie. Then swap.

  1. Plan 3 facts before you speak (2 real + 1 fake).
  2. Say all three in a flat tone — don't give away the lie with your voice.
  3. After the guess: You're right / You're wrong. The lie was ___.

Present Simple · the base you'll use every day

10 min · form · negatives · questions · travelling questions
Theory · form

Present Simple in one screen

Positive: I / you / we / they + verb · he / she / it + verb + s.

Negative: I / you / we / they + don't + verb · he / she / it + doesn't + verb.

Question: Do + I / you / we / they + verb? · Does + he / she / it + verb?

Use it for: habits, routines, facts. Signal words: every day / usually / often / sometimes / never.
