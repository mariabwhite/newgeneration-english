B · 1

Actually, I think… — the future of books

six question-cards · one for each future form
Actually, I think books will always be around — but the way we read them is going to change.my starter · steal it, argue with it, or replace it

Answer each question with a FULL sentence. Start with «Actually, I think…» or «It depends…». Notice which future form naturally lands in your answer.

1
Will paper books still exist in 100 years, or will everyone read on screens? → Simple Future · prediction
2
Look at kids with phones — is reading going to disappear? → to be going to · evidence in front of your eyes
3
What are you reading next month? Are you already planning a stack? → Present Continuous · fixed plan
4
This time next year, will you be reading a whole book in English? → Future Continuous · a moment in the future
5
By the time you finish school, how many books will you have read? → Future Perfect · completed by a moment
6
When you grow up, will you still buy paper books? → Simple Present in when-clause + Simple Future in main
My rule: no plain «yes/no» — always add because… or for example…. Minimum 2 sentences per card.

B · 3

Finish each sentence about YOUR real future

say your version out loud · then write it

Six starters about your real future. First say your version out loud, then write it as a full sentence.

  1. Next weekend I am … / am going to …  (a real plan you already have)
  2. By the time I turn 20, I will have …  (one thing you'll have finished or achieved)
  3. If I have free time this Sunday, I will …  (condition + result)
  4. This time tomorrow, I will be …-ing  (action in progress at a moment)
  5. When I finish this school year, I will …  (Present Simple in when-clause!)
  6. Honestly, I think books will … — because …  (prediction + one reason)

B · 5

Cheatsheet · 6 future forms · 30-second recall

read · close · say each form back with your own example
Block finale:
• will = prediction, promise, on-the-spot decision — I'll help you.
• going to = evidence in front of your eyes — Look, it's going to rain.
• Present Continuous = plan already in the diary — I'm meeting Anna at 5.
• Simple Present = timetables · AND after when / if / as soon as / before / after — The train leaves at 8. When I finish, I'll call.
• Future Continuous (will be -ing) = in the middle of an action at a moment — This time tomorrow I'll be flying.
• Future Perfect (will have + V3) = done BEFORE a moment in the future — By June I'll have read 10 books.
