Tell me about yourself · the classic warm-up

The safe, familiar way to start with strangers at work. Answer each question out loud in one short sentence. Tap ▶ to hear each question first.

5 basic questions · your real words · say them out loud
Tell me a bit about yourself.
Answer in 3 lines: name → work → city. That's it — no CV.
What's your name and where are you from?
"I'm Andrey, from Moscow. Originally from Kazan."
What do you do?
"I work in sales. I'm a sales manager at BestFood."
What company do you work for and what does it do?
"I work for BestFood. We sell frozen products to supermarkets in the Moscow region."
Why are you learning English?
"I need it for work. Sometimes I have foreign clients." · "For travel." · "For movies without subtitles."
Rule: nobody is testing your English yet. Just remember what you would say — in English, one short sentence per question — if someone at work asks you. Say each answer twice out loud.

📖 Reading · Andrey's first business trip

Read the short story once. Then answer 5 questions below.

Andrey's first business trip

A short story · ~140 words · about 1 minute

My name is Andrey and I'm a sales manager for a Moscow food company. Last month I had my first business trip abroad — a small conference in Prague.

The flight was three hours. I stayed at a nice hotel near the old town. On the first evening I was very nervous. My English is not perfect, and I didn't know anybody.

On the second day, during the coffee break, a German man came up to me. He said, "Hi, I'm Thomas. Where are you from?" We talked for ten minutes about our companies, then about our families, and finally about football. He was a big fan of Bayern Munich; I told him about Spartak.

By the end of the conference, we had exchanged business cards and Thomas invited me to visit Berlin. It was just small talk over coffee — but it opened a door.

Ex.1 · True / False / Not Stated · 5 items
1. Andrey works for a company in Prague.
He works for a Moscow food company; the conference was in Prague.
2. The flight took three hours.
"The flight was three hours."
3. On the first evening, Andrey felt nervous.
"I was very nervous. My English is not perfect."
4. Thomas is from Berlin.
The text says Thomas is German and a Bayern Munich fan, but never confirms Berlin as his hometown. He only invites Andrey to Berlin.
5. Andrey and Thomas exchanged business cards.
"By the end of the conference, we had exchanged business cards."

Quick check · grammar & vocab

10 MCQ · one attempt each. The correct answer appears after your click.

10 items · shuffled options
1. Nice ___ you.
"Nice to meet you" — fixed phrase for first meeting.
2. I ___ for a small IT company.
Present Simple for permanent job. "I" → work, no -s.
3. How ___ business?
"Business" is singular uncountable → is.
4. Have you ___ to Moscow before?
Present Perfect "have + been" for life experience.
5. Could you say that ___, please?
Word order: "again" + adverb "more slowly".
6. ___ you like some coffee?
"Would you like…?" — polite offer.
7. I ___ in the food industry.
"I work in the ___ industry" is the fixed phrase.
8. Last summer I ___ to Georgia.
"Last summer" = finished past → Past Simple "went".
9. Sorry — ___ a moment, please.
"Give me a moment" — natural. "Wait me" is a common mistake; correct is "wait for me".
10. Just to make sure I understand — you ___ we start next month?
"You mean" — fixed clarification phrase.

Ex.1 · 10 real client lines · pick the response
1. Client: "Hi, I don't think we've met. I'm Marco."
Standard first-meeting reply: repeat their name + introduce yourself.
2. Client: "So, what do you do exactly?"
One clear sentence: role + company + what the company does.
3. Client: "How was your trip?"
Polite, one line, positive tone — even if the flight was tough.
4. Client: "Would you like some coffee?"
"Yes, please" + how you take it. That's it.
5. Client: "Sorry, could you repeat that?"
Just repeat, slowly. No apology needed.
6. Client: "How long have you worked there?"
A number + "years now" or "months now" — that's the whole answer.
7. Client: "Have you been to Berlin before?"
Answer + bounce back a question. Keeps the small talk alive.
8. Client: "What brings you here today?"
Bridge to business — say the topic in one sentence.
9. Client: "Are you flying back tonight?"
Short factual answer. If you want, add one detail ("early flight").
10. Client: "Shall we get started?"
The end of small talk — a short "yes" is all you need.

🌍 Travel talk · a mini-block

Travel is the easiest small-talk topic in the world. Every foreigner asks. Here's the vocab, the pattern, and 6 speaking prompts.

Travel vocab · 12 words · tap chip to hear
Rule: in a first meeting, say ONE travel word and let the other person react. "I flew in yesterday, no delay this time." → they'll pick it up.
Ex.1 · "Have you ever been to...?" · 8 gap-fill
Pattern: "Have you ever been to [place]?" — Present Perfect for life experience. Answer: "Yes, I have. I went in [year]." OR "No, I haven't, but I'd love to."
  1. Have you ever to Italy?
  2. Yes, I . Twice, actually.
  3. Have you ever to Japan? — No, I .
  4. I to Turkey last summer with my family.
  5. Where did you for holidays last year?
  6. I've never abroad for work, only for holidays.
  7. Have you been to Moscow before?
  8. Next month I to Berlin for a short trip.
Ex.2 · 6 travel speaking prompts · say out loud
What's the best trip you've ever taken?
"The best trip was Georgia in 2023 — beautiful mountains, amazing food."
Which country would you love to visit and why?
"I'd love to visit Japan. I want to see Tokyo and try real sushi."
Do you prefer the sea or the mountains?
"Sea, definitely. I need to swim and relax on holiday."
Tell me about a funny travel story.
"Once I left my passport in the hotel safe. I remembered at the airport."
What do you usually pack for a business trip?
"One suit, three shirts, laptop, and a book for the flight."
How do you feel about long flights?
"I don't love them, but a good movie and coffee help."

Your summer in three photos

Think of three real photos from your last summer. Talk about each one in 2-3 sentences. Past Simple is fine, Present is fine too. Use the templates as a starter.

Photo 1 — a place
"This is a photo of ___. I went there in ___. It was ___."
Photo 2 — a person
"In this photo I can see ___. We were ___. He/she is ___."
Photo 3 — a moment
"This was the moment when ___. I remember ___. It made me feel ___."
Speaking tip: don't try to describe every detail. Two-three simple sentences per photo. This is how humans talk about photos — with feeling, not a report.
