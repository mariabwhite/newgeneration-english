Say it back — repeat after me

Six short phrases. Tap the play button, hear it once, say it back the same way. Notice the tone — English isn't monotone; it goes up and down.

1

2
"How was your day?" (curious, going up)
3

4

5

6

Tricky bits: "actually" = ACK-choo-lee (three syllables, not four) · "amazing" = uh-MAY-zing (stress in the middle) · "sounds" — the 'd' almost disappears in fast speech.

Your summer in three photos

describe each square · past simple is fine, present is fine too

Imagine three photos from your last month. One place. One person. One moment. Tell me about each — three or four sentences.

Photo 1A placewhere were you?
Photo 2A personwho was there?
Photo 3A momentwhat happened?
Guide questions — tap to hear:
  • Where were you? What did the place look like?
  • Who was with you? What are they like?
  • What happened? What did you do?
  • How did you feel about it?
  • Would you go back? Why or why not?
Sentence starters: The place was… · We went there because… · The best moment was when… · I felt really…

Model answer — steal the structure

Example · about one place

The place was a tiny café near my grandmother's house in the countryside. It has this one small window that looks straight into an apple tree, so the light inside is green. We went there every morning because my grandmother wanted her cappuccino. The best moment was when the owner remembered our order after two days. That was random and honestly quite sweet. I felt really at home there — even though I only knew it for a week.

Two mistakes to avoid

❌ "Yesterday I go to the sea."
Past = went, not go. Trigger words: yesterday, last week, this summer, on Monday.
❌ "I very like this place."
"Very" doesn't go before verbs. Use "really like", "like it a lot", or "love".

Listening — a girl's first week in Moscow

tap play · listen once · then answer True / False
Audio · ~50 seconds

Hi, I'm Lera. I moved to Moscow with my family last week. So — my first week was a whole thing. On Monday we found our new flat. It's small but the kitchen has a huge window and the view is honestly underrated. On Wednesday I got lost on the metro. I took the wrong line and ended up somewhere near the river. I actually loved it — the walk back was so chill. My mum was less chill about it. The food here is random — my neighbour brought us hot pastries with cabbage and I lowkey want them every day now. I'm not really into big cities usually, but Moscow in summer? I think I'm into it.

True or False?

1. Lera moved to Moscow last month.
2. Lera got lost on the metro on Wednesday.
3. Lera doesn't like big cities and Moscow is no exception.
4. Lera's neighbour brought them cabbage pastries.

Episode 2 — Lera calls her friend

Dialogue · ~40 seconds

Lera: Hey! You'll never guess where I ended up today.
Anya: Wait, where are you now?
Lera: Some tiny street near the river. I took the wrong metro line.
Anya: Are you lost? Should I open a map?
Lera: Honestly? I'm not lost. I'm just… walking. It's actually kind of nice here.
Anya: Only you, Lera. Only you would get lost and call it "kind of nice".
Lera: That's the point of Moscow, right? Getting a little lost is worth it.
Anya: Fine. Send me a photo. I want the view.

Answer in one sentence

1
How does Lera feel about being lost? Use one word from Section 6.
2
What does Anya want at the end of the call?
3
Retell the story in three sentences — use "got lost", "worth it", "chill".
Now you: tell me one thing from your first week somewhere new — use at least one word from Section 6.

10

Reading — one short paragraph, then talk

read out loud · tap any sentence to hear it back · four questions after
Text · ~80 words · read out loud

Why I like early mornings now.

I never used to be a morning person. Waking up was a whole thing. But this summer something changed. I started going to bed by eleven and getting up at seven. The city is quiet then. The light is soft. I have time for a slow breakfast, and my head is clear before anyone messages me. I still like nights — nights are for music and thinking. But mornings are for me. That's the difference.

Four quick questions

1
What changed for the writer this summer?
2
What does she like about early mornings? Name two things.
3
Does she still like nights? Why?
4
Are you a morning or a night person? Has that changed recently?
Three phrases from the text worth stealing: "was a whole thing" · "before anyone messages me" · "that's the difference"

Your turn — one paragraph

Copy the structure. Write or say one paragraph starting like this:

"I never used to be a ___ person. But this summer / this year / lately, something changed. I started ___ and ___. The ___ is ___. I still like ___ — ___ are for ___. But ___ are for me. That's the difference."

Now you ask me — three questions

your turn · anything you want to know · in English · I'll answer honestly

A good conversation goes both ways. I've asked you a lot today. Now you get three questions back. They can be about English, about me, about anything.

💡 Idea 1 — ask about something practical: "How long does it take to feel comfortable speaking?"
