Block B · Patterns · 8 request formulas

learn the shape, then plug your real need into it
1. Can I have…? — simple, direct.
Can I have a large latte, please?

2. Could I have…? — polite, formal.
Could I have some smaller bills, please?

3. Could you…? — asking someone else to do something.
Could you check my prescription, please?

4. Would you mind + V-ing? — very polite.
Would you mind opening the window?

5. I'd like to… — desire, decision.
I'd like to exchange 200 euros.

6. Could I also have…? — add-on request.
Could I also have some antihistamines?

7. Do you have…? — checking availability.
Do you have any dairy-free options?

8. What do you recommend for…? — asking advice.
What do you recommend for a sore throat?
Speak drill: I say a situation, you produce the right formula.
«Ask for a coffee» → Can I have a flat white, please?
«Ask the pharmacist about a rash» → What do you recommend for an itchy rash?
«Add a receipt to the order» → Could I also have a receipt?
«Ask if they accept card» → Do you have contactless payment?

Block C · Grammar refresh · 12 tenses + passive + modals + conditionals + used to

full grammar map · signal words · one example per form
PRESENT
Present Simple · every day, usually, always · «I drink tea every morning.»
Present Continuous · now, at the moment, this week · «Right now I am checking my schedule.»
Present Perfect · already, just, yet, ever, never, since, for · «I have already taken my medicine.»
Present Perfect Continuous · since Monday, for 2 hours, lately, all morning · «I have been feeling tired lately.»

PAST
Past Simple · yesterday, last week, ago, in 2020 · «She arrived yesterday.»
Past Continuous · while, when + past simple · «I was reading when the phone rang.»
Past Perfect · by the time, before, after, already (in the past) · «By 9 am I had checked everything.»
Past Perfect Continuous · for/since + past reference · «We had been living there for 10 years.»

FUTURE
Future Simple (will) · tomorrow, next week, soon · «I will call you tomorrow.»
Be going to · plans · «I am going to start a new routine.»
Future Continuous · at this time next week · «This time tomorrow I will be flying to Toronto.»
Future Perfect · by + future time · «By June, I will have finished the course.»

OTHER CORE
Passive Voice · «Useful information is provided daily.» · «A corporate event was organised last week.»
Modals · should · must · can · could · would · «Employees should take a break.»
Zero Conditional · «If it rains, streets get wet.»
Conditional 1 (real) · «If I feel tired, I will rest.»
Conditional 2 (unreal) · «If I moved to Canada, I would take my books.»
Used to · past habits vs now · «I used to feel overwhelmed. Now I don't.»
Wish + Past Simple · «I wish my schedule were less complicated.»

Block B · Patterns · 6 formulas to describe a person

the sentence shapes that run this whole tab
1. At first, this person looks / seems / appears [adj], but actually [adj].
He looks strict, but actually he is very fair.

2. In stressful situations, they [verb + adj].
In stressful situations, she stays calm and focused.

3. They make me feel [emotion].
Calm people make me feel comfortable.

4. They make my day / situations / work [adjective].
Loud people make my day stressful. Supportive colleagues make work easier.

5. [Person] looks [adj], although [contrasting adj].
He looks tired-looking, although he never complains.

6. [Person] is [adj], but still [adj].
She is quiet, but still very confident.
Full model — a manager My manager looks well-dressed and confident. At first, she seems strict, but she is actually very fair. In stressful situations, she stays calm and focused, although the team feels overwhelmed. She makes me feel structured and supported.

Block C · Grammar refresh · «Make + person + feel + adj» + contrast connectors

the two grammar patterns that describe people
Pattern «make + person + feel + adjective»
Calm people make me feel comfortable.
Loud people make situations complicated.
Supportive colleagues make work easier.
⚠ Never use «to» here — NOT «make me to feel». It is make + person + bare infinitive / adj.

Contrast connectors
but — direct contrast · He looks strict, but he is fair.
although / though — softer contrast · She is quiet, although she is confident.
still — «even so» · He is tired, still supportive.
however — formal · He looks confident. However, he feels insecure inside.
yet — surprise · She is strict, yet very kind.

Block B · Patterns · 5 sentence shapes for personal narrative

memorise the shape, plug in your real story
1. Past habit vs now:
«I used to [verb], but now I [verb].»
I used to feel overwhelmed by change. Now I embrace it.

2. Ongoing lately (Present Perfect Continuous):
«I have been [verb-ing] lately / for X / since Y.»
I've been thinking about Canada for months.

3. Unreal hypothetical (Conditional 2):
«If I [past simple], I would [verb].»
If I moved to Canada tomorrow, I would take my grandmother's ring.

4. Reflection with «that's»:
«A year ago I couldn't. Now I can. That's the difference.»

5. Two-sentence transition:
«Something changed. I started [verb-ing]…»
Something changed this year. I started saying yes to things that scared me.

Block C · Grammar refresh · Used to · PPC · Conditional 2

three grammar mountains that make personal English come alive
USED TO · past habit that has now stopped
+ I used to smoke.
– I didn't use to like coffee. (NOT «used not to»)
? Did you use to live here? (NOT «did you used to»)
⚠ Only past. Never present. «I use to go» is wrong.

PRESENT PERFECT CONTINUOUS · action from past → now, often ongoing
+ I have been learning English for 3 years.
– She has not been sleeping well lately.
? How long have you been living here?
Signal words: for X · since Y · lately · all morning · recently

CONDITIONAL 2 · unreal / hypothetical present
Formula: If + Past Simple, would + V
If I had more time, I would travel more.
If I were you, I would take the risk. («were» for all persons in formal use)
⚠ Not real, not future — imagined.

Block D · Reading · «Something changed this year»

read out loud · then TF + MCQ + match
Text · 150 words · B1 register · reflective essay

A year ago, I used to feel that big changes were something that happened to other people. My routine was familiar, my city was familiar, and the idea of moving abroad felt unrealistic — a story other families lived, not mine.

Something changed this year. I don't know exactly when. I started saying yes to things that scared me. I started studying English more seriously. I've been reading in English for about six months now, and last week I finished my first English book without a dictionary.

If someone had told me a year ago that I would be moving to Canada with my family in six months, I would have laughed. Nervously, but I would have laughed. Now, I look at my suitcase in the corner and I think: this is not other families' story anymore. This is ours.
