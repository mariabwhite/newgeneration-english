Have got + 's — who belongs to whom

10 min · theory · 2 drills · 1 builder
Theory · 2 min

have got — "I have"

In British English, have got means «I have». It's shorter than I have.

  • I have got a sister. (I've got)
  • You have got a brother. (You've got)
  • He / she / it has got a pet. (He's got / She's got)
  • We / they have got a big family. (We've got / They've got)

Rule: only he / she / it uses has. Everyone else uses have.

Negative: I haven't got a cousin. She hasn't got a brother.

Theory · 1 min

's — possessive (whose?)

To say "whose", add 's to the person's name or role:

  • Wendy's mother = Wendy's mother
  • Dracula's castle = Dracula's castle
  • my brother's pet = my brother's pet

Common mistake: The mother of Wendy — wrong. Say Wendy's mother.

🏛 Cultural note from the book · Victorian family

200 years ago in England…

In Victorian England (Queen Victoria, 1837–1901) family life was very strict. Papa went to work, mama stayed at home. Children had to be «seen but not heard» — you could see them but not hear them! Imagine: at the dinner table you can't say a single word, only listen to the adults. Hard, right? 😅

12.1 · Grammar · Present Tenses · Destination B1 reference

Present Simple · Present Continuous · Present Perfect · Present Perfect Continuous

Four tenses, one Monster Family. Each tense gets a Form table, a Use list, Time markers, and Monster examples. Then a side-by-side comparison and five drills.

Unit 1 · Present Simple
Form + affirmative − negative ? question
I / you / we / theylivedo not (don't) liveDo … live?
he / she / itlives (-s / -es / -ies)does not (doesn't) liveDoes … live?
Use
  • Permanent situations & general facts — Vampires live in Transylvania.
  • Habits & routines — Dracula drinks blood every night.
  • Timetables & schedules — The ghost tour starts at midnight.
  • Permanent states — I know Aunt Morticia very well.
Time markers

always · usually · often · sometimes · rarely · never · every day/week/Halloween · once a month · on Mondays

Unit 2 · Present Continuous
Form + affirmative − negative ? question
Iam (I'm) carvingam not (I'm not) carvingAm I carving?
he / she / itis ('s) carvingis not (isn't) carvingIs … carving?
you / we / theyare ('re) carvingare not (aren't) carvingAre … carving?
Use
  • Actions happening right now — Morticia is cooking spider soup.
  • Temporary situations — Pugsley is staying with us this week.
  • Changing situations — The full moon is rising fast.
  • Future arrangements — We are meeting at the castle on Saturday.
  • Annoying habits with «always» — Wednesday is always breaking mirrors!
Time markers

now · right now · at the moment · today · this week/month · currently · Look! · Listen!

⚠️ State verbs — NOT used in Continuous

like · love · hate · know · understand · remember · believe · want · need · see · hear · own · belong — these go in Simple, not Continuous.
❌ I am knowing Aunt Hilda.  ✅ I know Aunt Hilda.

Unit 3 · Present Perfect
Form + affirmative − negative ? question
I / you / we / theyhave ('ve) brokenhave not (haven't) brokenHave … broken?
he / she / ithas ('s) brokenhas not (hasn't) brokenHas … broken?
Use
  • Past action with a result in the present — Wednesday has broken three mirrors (so now the castle has none).
  • Life experience (ever / never) — I have never seen a friendly ghost.
  • Recent actions (just, recently, lately) — Aunt Hilda has just got married.
  • Unfinished time period (today, this week/month/year) — Grandma has made 20 pies this week.
  • Already / yet / still — Have you finished your costume yet? — I have already finished it.
Time markers

ever · never · just · already · yet · still · recently · lately · today · this week/month/year · so far · for 2 years · since 2020

Unit 4 · Present Perfect Continuous
Form + affirmative − negative ? question
I / you / we / theyhave been bakinghave not been bakingHave … been baking?
he / she / ithas been bakinghas not been bakingHas … been baking?
Use
  • Duration up to now — how long an action has been in progress — Grandma has been baking pies for three hours.
  • Recent continuous action with a visible result — I'm tired — I have been carving pumpkins all afternoon.
  • Repeated action over a period — The werewolf has been howling every night this week.
Time markers

for 2 hours · since Monday · all day · all week · recently · lately · how long?

12.4 · Writing · 100-word full Monster-family portrait

Write a paragraph «My Monster Family Reunion» · 100 words

Imagine your Monster Family is gathering for a reunion this Saturday. In 100 words describe who's coming, what they are doing right now, what they have already done, and how long they have been doing it. Use all four present tenses.

✅ Checklist · tick each point
  • At least 5 family words from blocks 5-9 above (e.g. cousins · in-laws · ancestors · bride · stepfather · descendants · twins · fiancée)
  • At least 1 Present Simple (general fact about the family)
  • At least 1 Present Continuous (what they are doing right now)
  • At least 1 Present Perfect (with already / just / ever / never / this week)
  • At least 1 Present Perfect Continuous (with for … / since …)
  • A paragraph, not a bullet list. Start with: «My Monster Family is huge…»
🎯 Model · 100-word sample

My Monster Family is huge. We have lots of close and distant relatives — witches, vampires and even a ghost stepfather. Every Halloween we celebrate our ancestors' recipe for pumpkin pie. Right now my aunt is sewing a bat costume for my cousin, and the twins are flying over from Transylvania. My grandma has already made 20 pies this week and she has been baking since Tuesday — her kitchen smells wonderful! My niece has just got engaged to a werewolf, and the wedding will be in December. I love my monster family, in-laws and all. (101 words)

12.5 · Speaking · three formats · speak out loud

From description to role-play

Three different speaking tasks — in increasing difficulty. 60-90 seconds each — record a voice note and send on Telegram.

A · Describe · the reunion photo · 60 sec

Imagine a big family photo: 15 monsters in Grandma Munster's living room. Describe: I can see… Right now they are… In the front there is… My favourite monster in the photo is…

  • Use at least 3 Present Continuous (who is doing what in the photo)
  • Use at least 3 family words from blocks 5-9
B · Role-play · telephone call · 90 sec

You are Zhenya. Grandma Munster is calling to ask who is coming to the reunion. Answer all 4 of her questions using Present Perfect (who has already confirmed) and Present Perfect Continuous (what people have been doing before the call):

  1. «Who has already said they're coming?»
  2. «Has anyone recently got married or divorced in the family?»
  3. «What have the cousins been doing this October?»
  4. «How long have you been preparing your costume?»
C · Monologue · «My Monster Family Tree» · 90 sec

Tell about your monster-family tree. Start with ancestors (who lived 500 years ago), then current family (parents · siblings · in-laws), then future descendants (what you think your monster grandchildren will be).

  • At least 6 family words from blocks 5-9
  • At least 1 example of each of the 4 present tenses
  • Beginning-middle-end — not chaos, a structure (past ancestors → present family → future descendants)
