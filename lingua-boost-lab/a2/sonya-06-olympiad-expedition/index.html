THE DETECTIVE’S TOOLKIT

Method first, answers second

Aa

Meaning, not matching words

The same word in the question and the recording does not prove the answer. What matters is who is speaking, about whom, and with what meaning.

all sports ≠ dangerous sports

T / F / ?

Three different decisions

True: the text confirms it. False: the text contradicts it. Not Stated: the text does not decide.

No evidence ≠ False

← gap →

Look both ways

Clues live on both sides of the gap: article, preposition, tense, number, part of speech.

very + skilfully + prepared

1 → 2 → 3

Keep the paragraph focused

Find the main idea of the paragraph. A true, interesting fact may still be off topic.

A fact can be true and off topic.

Try the method · preparation practice

Listening routine · before / during / after
  1. Read the questions. Underline names, numbers and words such as all, only, never.
  2. Listen for the main idea first. Write a short note, not a complete sentence.
  3. Listen again for evidence and contradictions.
  4. Use the script after your attempt. Find the exact phrase that decides the answer.

GRAMMAR BRIDGE

From familiar tenses to olympiad details

Passive voice

be + V3. The tense sits on be, the action lives in V3.

Europa is covered in ice.
The city was planned in 1682.

Lie / lay · rise / raise

No object: lie (recline), rise (move up). With an object: lay (put down), raise (lift up).

The papers lie here.
I lay the papers here.
The moon rises. She raises her hand.

Word formation

Decide the part of speech first; then meaning, number and spelling.

fair → unfair
improve → improvements
skill → skilfully / skillfully

Extra-word editing

Read the whole sentence. Check whether it already has a main verb before adding another verb form.

Grabar was discovered a motif.
He discovered it.

USE WHAT YOU DISCOVERED

Speaking studio

Extra practice on the route topics. These tasks do not count toward the official score.

01 · Museum curator

Choose one exhibit from the museum dialogue. Give a 45-second introduction: what it is, where its original is, and why it interests you.

Possible answer · after your attempt

I would choose the Gold of Troy. The dialogue connects it with Heinrich Schliemann and his childhood dream. I would explain how a story can inspire someone to keep learning. I would like to know more about the objects and how they were discovered.

02 · Agree, disagree, explain

“Dogs should always be on a leash in public spaces.” Give an opinion, one reason and one respectful response to a different opinion.

Possible answer · after your attempt

I think dogs should be on a leash in busy public places because some people are afraid of them. I understand that dogs need exercise, but towns could provide safe areas where they can run freely.

03 · Sell it, then question it

Give a 30-second bicycle advertisement. Then become the buyer and ask three questions that test its claims.

Possible answer · after your attempt

This bicycle has a light frame and a comfortable seat. It could be useful for short journeys. Buyer: How much does it weigh? What does the guarantee cover? Can I try it before buying it?

04 · History guide

Compare the two Moscow residences using only the script. Include a date, a former owner and a cultural connection.

Possible answer · after your attempt

The British Ambassador’s residence was designed in 1893 and belonged to Pavel Kharitonenko. Spaso House was built in 1913 and belonged to Nikolay Vtorov. The script connects Spaso House with Bulgakov and a nearby church with a painting by Polenov.

WORKBOOK · TRANSFER THE SKILL

Write, explain, remember

Writing · A letter to the editor

Write 100–130 words about a public space in your town. Explain a problem, give two reasons for your opinion and suggest a fair solution. Use a polite opening and ending. This is additional practice, not an official writing task.

Plan: position → reason 1 → reason 2 → solution → polite ending.

0 words

Sample letter · after writing

Dear Editor,

I am writing about the small field near our school. Some people want to close it to dogs, but I believe there is a better solution.

First, many families use the field to exercise their pets. It is one of the few open spaces in our neighbourhood. Second, responsible owners should not lose access because a few people ignore the rules.

However, children and other visitors also need to feel safe. I suggest creating a separate area for dogs and asking owners to clean up after them. Clear signs would help everyone understand the rules.

I hope the council will consider a solution that works for the whole community.

Yours faithfully,
A local student
