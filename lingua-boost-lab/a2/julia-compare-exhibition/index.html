2

Spelling exceptions for short adjectives

Theory · 2
When you add -er / -est, the spelling sometimes changes. Four short rules cover 99% of cases.
2.1 · Ends in silent -e → add just -r / -st

If the word ends in -e, don't double it. Just -r or -st.

BaseComparativeSuperlative
nicenicerthe nicest
largelargerthe largest
safesaferthe safest

✗ niceer · largeer   ✓ nicer · larger

2.2 · CVC → double the final consonant

CVC = consonant + vowel + consonant (one of each, at the end). Double the last consonant, then add -er / -est.

Base (CVC)ComparativeSuperlative
big (b-i-g)biggerthe biggest
hot (h-o-t)hotterthe hottest
thin (th-i-n)thinnerthe thinnest
fat (f-a-t)fatterthe fattest
Watch out ↳ if there are two vowels or two consonants at the end, do NOT double.
clean (two vowels e-a) → cleaner, cleanner.
cold (two consonants l-d) → colder, coldder.
cheap → cheaper, cheapper.
-w and -x are special: we never double them.
new → newer, newwer.   low → lower, lowwer.
2.3 · Ends in -y after a consonant → change -y to -i

If the word ends in -y and before the -y is a consonant → replace -y with -i, then add -er / -est. This covers many 2-syllable adjectives.

BaseComparativeSuperlative
happyhappierthe happiest
busybusierthe busiest
easyeasierthe easiest
prettyprettierthe prettiest
lazylazierthe laziest
heavyheavierthe heaviest

✗ happyer · more happy · the happyest   ✓ happier · the happiest

2.4 · Two-syllable borderline — some can go either way

A few 2-syllable adjectives work with both patterns (short and long): clever · narrow · simple · quiet · gentle · polite.

BaseShorter formLonger form
clevercleverermore clever
quietquietermore quiet
simplesimplermore simple
At B1 safe rule ↳ for these borderline words, use the shorter form (cleverer, quieter, simpler). Both are correct, but shorter is more common in speech.
3

Long adjectives & five patterns

Theory · 3
Long adjectives always take more / most. On top of that there are 5 sentence patterns you need to know by B1.
3.1 · Long = more / most

2 or more syllables (and NOT ending in -y after consonant) → always more / most. Never add -er.

BaseComparativeSuperlative
beautifulmore beautifulthe most beautiful
interestingmore interestingthe most interesting
difficultmore difficultthe most difficult
expensivemore expensivethe most expensive
importantmore importantthe most important
3.2 · Pattern 1 · Comparative + than

We compare two things. Word order: A is  [comp]  than B.

The elephant is bigger than the lion.
Mum's pancakes are better than the shop ones.
The peacock is more beautiful than the pigeon.

3.3 · Pattern 2 · the + superlative + in / of

Superlative = one out of a group. Always with the. Group goes after: in (place) or of (people / things).

The giraffe is the tallest animal in the zoo.
Barsik is the best cat in the whole world.
She is the youngest of three sisters.

Don't drop the: Giraffe is tallest animal → The giraffe is the tallest animal.
3.4 · Pattern 3 · as … as (equal) and not as … as (less)

Same quality — as + base + as. Less — not as + base + as (= less than).

John is as fast as his sister.  (same speed)
The pigeon is not as beautiful as the peacock.  (less beautiful)
Today is not as hot as yesterday.  (less hot)

Mini-trick ↳ not as X as and less X than mean the same thing.
The pigeon is not as beautiful as the peacock = The pigeon is less beautiful than the peacock.
3.5 · Pattern 4 · Double comparative — and

Something changes step by step — comp + and + comp. For long adjectives: more and more + base.

The carousel went faster and faster.
Barsik is getting bigger and bigger every month.
The exhibition becomes more and more popular every year.

3.6 · Pattern 5 · the + comp, the + comp (the more … the better)

One thing grows → another thing grows (or shrinks).

The more you practise, the better you speak.
The hotter the day, the slower Barsik walks.
The older John gets, the more interesting his questions become.
