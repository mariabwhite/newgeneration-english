Model answer — steal the structure

Example · about one character

The character I never forget is Sirius Black. On the first pages he looks like the villain — everyone thinks he betrayed Harry's parents and killed thirteen people. He escaped from the darkest wizard prison in the world, which nobody had done before. But by the end of the book we find out he is innocent. He is Harry's godfather. He spent twelve years in Azkaban for a crime he didn't commit. That reversal completely got me. Sirius is powerful because he is broken and kind at the same time.

Two common mistakes about books

❌ "The book is written by Rowling in 1999. It's very interesting."
Past events → Past Simple: "Rowling wrote it in 1999." Present opinion → Present Simple: "It's very interesting" ✓
❌ "This author writes very good."
"Good" is an adjective. After a verb use the adverb "well": "This author writes very well." Or better: "This author is a very good writer."

Listening — Sirius Black tells his story

tap play · listen once · then answer True / False · then the dialogue
Episode 1 · ~55 seconds · Sirius Black speaks (first person)

My name is Sirius Black. Twelve years ago I was sent to Azkaban — the darkest wizard prison in the world. Everyone believed I had betrayed my best friends, James and Lily Potter. They said I gave them to Voldemort. They said I killed thirteen people in one street. But I didn't. The real traitor was a small, forgotten man called Peter Pettigrew. For twelve years he hid from me, in a form no one would ever suspect. I sat in Azkaban and I thought about it, every day. And when I finally saw his face in a newspaper, I did something no one had ever done before. I escaped. Not to hurt Harry. To protect him.

True or False?

1. Sirius Black spent twenty years in Azkaban.
2. Everybody in the wizarding world believed Sirius was a killer.
3. The real traitor was Peter Pettigrew.
4. Sirius escaped from Azkaban to hurt Harry.

Reading — the train to Hogwarts (Chapter 5)

read out loud · tap any sentence to hear it · four questions after
Text · ~120 words · adapted for A2+ · read out loud

Something on the train.

The train slowed down. It shouldn't have slowed down — they were not at Hogwarts yet. The rain got heavier. The lights went out. It was completely dark. Then the door opened. Something stood there. Tall. In a black cloak. Harry couldn't see a face. Just a grey hand, wet and dead-looking, coming out of the cloak. The air became cold. Ice-cold. Harry felt something he had never felt before — like every happy thing he had ever known was leaving him. He heard a scream. A woman screaming. He thought it was his mother. Then everything went black. When Harry opened his eyes, Professor Lupin was next to him, holding a piece of chocolate. "Eat this," Lupin said. "It will help."

Four quick questions

1
Why is it strange that the train slowed down?
2
What does Harry feel when the Dementor comes close?
3
Whose voice does Harry hear? Why is that important?
4
Who helps Harry at the end? What does he give him?
Three phrases from the text worth stealing: "the lights went out" · "the air became cold" · "everything went black"

Word focus — collocations

to slow down
= reduce speed · train slowed down, my heart slowed down
ice-cold
very strong «cold» · works for weather, hands, atmosphere
everything went black
= a person lost consciousness · classic English phrase for fainting

Grammar reference — 12 tenses cheatsheet

click to open · keep this open on the second screen while we talk · not a lecture, a map
📖 All 12 English tenses — formula · sign words · example
TenseFormulaSignal wordsExample
Present
Present Simple I / you / we / they work
he / she / it works		 every day, usually, always, often, sometimes, on Mondays I walk to school every day.
Present Continuous am / is / are + V-ing now, right now, at the moment, currently, today She is studying for her exams at the moment.
Present Perfect have / has + V3 (past participle) already, just, yet, ever, never, since, for, so far, recently They have already visited that museum.
Present Perfect Continuous have / has been + V-ing for, since, all day, lately, all morning We have been waiting for the bus for over an hour.
Past
Past Simple V2 (regular -ed, irregular list) yesterday, last week, in 2020, ago, when I was 10 He played soccer with his friends yesterday.
Past Continuous was / were + V-ing while, when (long action interrupted), at 5 pm yesterday, all day yesterday She was reading a book when the phone rang.
Past Perfect had + V3 by the time, before, after, already (in the past), when + past simple They had already finished their dinner when I arrived.
Past Perfect Continuous had been + V-ing for / since + past reference, all night before We had been studying all night before the exam.
Future
Future Simple will + V
(or) be going to + V		 tomorrow, next week, in 2030, soon, one day I will go to the party tomorrow.
Future Continuous will be + V-ing at this time next week, this time tomorrow, all day tomorrow They will be travelling to Europe next month.
Future Perfect will have + V3 by + future time, by then, by the end of the week She will have completed her project by the end of the week.
Future Perfect Continuous will have been + V-ing by X (for Y hours / years), by next year for ten years By next year we will have been living in this city for ten years.
