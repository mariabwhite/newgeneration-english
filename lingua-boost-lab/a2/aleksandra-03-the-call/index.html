Ordering at a café + asking for Wi-Fi

8 min · menu · polite phrases · Wi-Fi questions · fill-in drills
Theory

How to order without pointing

Could I have a cappuccino, please? is the safest opener anywhere. I'd like a croissant. is a shorter version — just as polite. The barista may ask Anything else? — reply That's all, thank you. To pay, ask How much is it?

Rule: always end with please or thank you. In Paris, learning Merci gets you a smile. In English, Thanks a lot! works too.

New today — Wi-Fi: Is there Wi-Fi here? is the simple opener. If they say yes, add Could I have the Wi-Fi password, please? Same polite pattern as the order itself.

Present Simple · Do you…? / Where does…?

10 min · questions · short answers · word order
Theory · question form

Two question openers · nothing else

I / you / we / they: Do + subject + verb?

He / she / it: Does + subject + verb (no -s on the verb — does takes it).

Examples:

  • Do you like coffee? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  • Does Marco live in Paris? — No, he doesn't. He lives in Milan.
  • Where do you work? — I work in marketing.
  • What does she do? — She's a shop assistant.

Common mistake: Does he lives in Milan? — wrong. Correct: Does he live in Milan?

Homework · before next lesson

Four short tasks, about 20 minutes

  1. Café + Wi-Fi script — write your own café order using at least 4 phrases from Section 2 (Could I have…, Anything else?, Is there Wi-Fi here?, Could I have the Wi-Fi password, please?, How much is it?). Send it on Telegram.
  2. Directions — imagine you're in your town and a tourist asks you the way to the pharmacy. Write 3 sentences of directions using turn left / right, go straight, opposite, next to.
  3. Voice note · 60 seconds — retell Sasha's week in Paris in your own words. Use Present Simple (She lives / drinks / writes…). Send the audio.
  4. Present Simple questions — write 8 questions to ask a new colleague (4 with Do you…?, 4 with Where / What / When does…?). Watch the word order.

Bonus: next time you buy a coffee anywhere — order and ask for Wi-Fi in English out loud (even to yourself). Report back next lesson how it felt.
