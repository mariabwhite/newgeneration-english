Ordering at a café

8 min · menu · polite phrases · fill-in drills
Theory

How to order without pointing

Could I have a cappuccino, please? is the safest opener anywhere. I'd like a croissant. is a shorter version — just as polite. The barista may ask Anything else? — reply That's all, thank you. To pay, ask How much is it?

Rule: always end with please or thank you. In Italy, learning Grazie gets you a smile. In English, Thanks a lot! works too.

Asking for directions

7 min · 8 direction words · polite openers · MCQ
Theory

Stop a stranger the polite way

Always open with Excuse me, … — never Hey!. The two safest questions: Where is the Trevi Fountain? and How do I get to the Trevi Fountain? If you need to know distance, ask Is it far? A friendly ending: Thanks a lot!

Answer pattern: direction + landmark + time. Turn right, then it's on your left. Five minutes on foot.

Numbers 1-100 + prices

6 min · reference · prices · time · listen & type
Theory

Reading prices & times out loud

Prices: €4.50 → "four euros fifty". €12 → "twelve euros". €1 → "one euro" (no s).

Time: 10:30 → "half past ten" or "ten thirty". 7:15 → "quarter past seven". 9:00 → "nine o'clock".

Common trap: 13 (thirteen) vs 30 (thirty) — the stress moves. Thirty vs thirteen.

8

Present Simple · Do you…? / Where does…?

10 min · questions · short answers · word order
Theory · question form

Two question openers · nothing else

I / you / we / they: Do + subject + verb?

He / she / it: Does + subject + verb (no -s on the verb — does takes it).

Examples:

  • Do you like coffee? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  • Does Marco live in Rome? — No, he doesn't. He lives in Milan.
  • Where do you work? — I work in marketing.
  • What does she do? — She's a shop assistant.

Common mistake: Does he lives in Milan? — wrong. Correct: Does he live in Milan?
