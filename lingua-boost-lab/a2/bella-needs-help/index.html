Bella Needs Help · pharmacy, doctor, emergency

It's Bella's third day in Canada. Yesterday was wonderful — coffee, a SIM card, a long walk by the lake. But this morning she woke up with a strong headache. We follow her to Shoppers Drug Mart, then to a walk-in clinic, and finally we hear how she helps a stranger who needs to call 911.

Pharmacy Doctor 911 call "Can I…?" + "Could you…?"

Today's only grammar focus · asking politely

When something is wrong, you need polite help. Three magic openings. Every scene below uses them.

Can I have…? (asking to receive) Could you…? (asking the other person) I need… (saying what you need)
"Can I have something for a headache, please?"
"Could you help me, please? I don't feel well."
"I need an ambulance. My friend is sick."

Scene 1 · at the pharmacyShoppers Drug Mart

Bella's headache won't go away. She walks into the nearest pharmacy.
💊
Bella stands at the pharmacy counter. She rubs her forehead. The pharmacist, a friendly woman, looks up and smiles. "Good morning! How can I help you?"
Step 1 · Vocabulary
Step 2 · Dialogue · read out loud (Bella → you)

Pharmacist: Good morning! How can I help you?

Bella: Hi. I have a strong headache. Can I have something for it, please?

Pharmacist: Of course. Do you prefer pills or syrup?

Bella: Pills, please. Something simple, without a prescription.

Pharmacist: Try these. One in the morning, one in the evening. Two times a day.

Bella: Could you write it down for me, please?

Pharmacist: Sure. Here you are. The cash desk is on your right.

Bella: Thank you very much.

Step 3 · Controlled drill · choose the right word
Step 4 · Your turn · use the template

You feel a little ill. Choose your own symptom.

Hi. I have ____ (a headache / a sore throat / a cough / a fever). Can I have ____ (something for it / some medicine), please? ____ (Pills / Syrup / Spray), please. Could you write down how to take them? Thank you very much.
Say it three times out loud — first reading, second smoother, third with eye contact.

Scene 2 · at the walk-in clinicDoctor's office

The headache is back the next day, plus a sore throat. Bella goes to a walk-in clinic without an appointment.
🩺
Bella sits in the doctor's office. The nurse called her name. The doctor looks at her, smiles, and asks: "Hello, what brings you in today?"
Step 1 · Vocabulary
Step 2 · Dialogue · read out loud (Bella → you)

Doctor: Hello, what brings you in today?

Bella: Hi. I have a headache and a sore throat. I don't feel well.

Doctor: When did it start?

Bella: Yesterday morning. I took some pills, but it didn't help much.

Doctor: Do you have a fever? Let me check.

Bella: I think so. I feel hot and tired.

Doctor: It looks like a small cold. Rest, drink hot tea, take these for three days.

Bella: Could you write me a prescription, please?

Doctor: Of course. Here you are. Feel better soon!

Step 3 · Controlled drill · choose the right word
Step 4 · Your turn · use the template

You go to the doctor. Choose your own symptoms.

Hi. I have ____ (a headache / a sore throat / a fever / a cough). I don't feel well. It started ____ (yesterday / two days ago / this morning). Could you write me a prescription, please? Thank you very much.
Three times out loud — Elena, slow is fine. The doctor is patient.

Scene 3 · calling 911Emergency

On her way home, Bella sees an older man fall on the sidewalk. She calls 911 — the Canadian emergency number.
🚨
Bella is on Queen Street. An older man is on the ground, holding his chest. People stop, but no one acts. Bella takes her phone and calls 911.
Step 1 · Vocabulary
Step 2 · Dialogue · read out loud (Bella → you)

Operator: 911, what is your emergency?

Bella: I need an ambulance, please. There is a man on the street. He fell down.

Operator: Where are you, ma'am?

Bella: I'm on Queen Street, near the post office. The man is holding his chest.

Operator: Is he conscious? Is he breathing?

Bella: Yes, he is breathing. But he looks very weak.

Operator: Help is on the way. Could you stay with him until they arrive?

Bella: Yes, I'll stay. Please hurry.

Step 3 · Controlled drill · choose the right word
Step 4 · Your turn · use the template

You see an emergency. Call 911. Use four short sentences.

I need ____ (an ambulance / the police / the fire department), please. There is ____ (a man / a woman / a child) ____ (on the street / in the building / by the lake). I'm on ____ (___ Street, near ___). ____ (He / She) is breathing but ____ (very weak / unconscious / hurt). Please hurry.
Practice slow + clear. In emergency English, calm voice = fast help.
