B1 Speaking · Part 01 · Boarding

China Trip · Survival Talk

You are flying to China. You will land in a huge airport, take a taxi where no one speaks English, check into a hotel, order dinner and get lost at least twice. This lab gives you the exact phrases for each of those moments — so you speak first and don't freeze.

Level
B1 · teen traveller
Duration
~75 minutes
Focus
Airport · Taxi · Hotel · Food
Goal
Speak first · translate later
01

Trip check · warm up your mouth

Answer each card out loud in 3–4 full sentences. No half-answers. Push through even if a word is missing — use around-the-word English.

Why we speak first: in China you will meet English at level A1–A2 in taxis, small hotels and markets. Your English will feel very high in comparison. Your job today is to unlock your mouth, not to translate perfectly.
Card 01
Where in China are you flying, and for how many days?
City · dates · who you go with · what you want to see most.
Card 02
What are you a little afraid of on this trip?
Language · food · getting lost · money · airport. One thing, one sentence.
Card 03
One thing you already know how to say in Chinese — say it now.
Hello, thank you, sorry, how much. Anything. Say it aloud.
02

Vocab · airport & immigration

Twelve airport words. Click ▶ on each one to hear a British voice.

Micro-theory · listen for the question word first. An officer's questions are short: Purpose? How long? Where staying? Return ticket? Catch the question word, not every syllable. Your answer can be one clean sentence.
boarding pass
The paper (or phone screen) they scan at the gate.
immigration · passport control
The desk with the officer and the stamp.
customs
The green / red channel after you get your bag.
to declare
"Do you have anything to declare?" — food, medicine, big money.
a return ticket
Your ticket back. They may ask about it.
a purpose (of visit)
Tourism · study · visiting family · business.
to stay at
"I'm staying at the Hilton Beijing." Not in, not on — at.
a QR code
Nearly everything in China is a QR code. Scan first, ask later.
a taxi rank
The line of taxis just outside the arrival hall.
a ride-hailing app
Didi = the Chinese Uber.
a subway
The underground / metro. Cheapest way into town.
lost
"I'm lost. Could you help me?"
03

Sentence frames · answers you already have

Learn these six frames by heart. When the officer speaks — you plug in your details and go.

Rule: full sentences, not one word. "Tourism." sounds nervous. "The purpose of my visit is tourism." sounds calm. Same info, different feeling.
Q · purpose
"The purpose of my visit is tourism / to visit my family."
Never just "sightseeing" — say the noun.
Q · how long
"I'm staying for ten days, until the twentieth of October."
Number + month. Repeat the date if he frowns.
Q · where staying
"I'm staying at the Grand Beijing Hotel in Chaoyang district."
Name of hotel + district. Have it on your phone.
Q · return ticket
"Yes, my return flight is on the twentieth of October."
Show the screenshot if he wants proof.
Q · alone?
"I'm travelling with my mother / family / a friend."
Or: "I'm travelling alone."
Q · declare?
"No, I have nothing to declare."
If yes: "Yes, I have some medicine for personal use."

05

Vocab · taxi & Didi

Most drivers speak no English. You need seven survival phrases and a phone with the hotel address in Chinese.

Micro-theory · the address trick. Save the Chinese name of your hotel as a screenshot before you land. Show it. Point. Don't try to pronounce it — just show and say "please".
"To this address, please."
Golden phrase — plus show your phone.
"How much is it, more or less?"
Ask before you get in. Show fingers if needed.
"Could you turn on the meter, please?"
Meter = the little box with a price. If it's off, the price will jump.
"Stop here, please."
Point out of the window. "Here, thank you."
"Can I pay by WeChat?"
Most drivers want WeChat / Alipay QR. Cash is a backup.
"Could I get a receipt?"
Useful for expenses and complaints.
Didi · a ride-hailing app
Chinese Uber. Wi-Fi + local number needed, or the English "Didi" app.
the subway · line 2 · exit A
Cheapest transport. Signs are in English too.

07

Vocab · hotel check-in

Eight phrases from the front desk. The clerk usually speaks basic English — you speak slowly and simply back. Click ▶.

Micro-theory · slow, not loud. If they don't understand, don't say it louder. Say it slower and shorter. "I have a booking" is better than "Well, I actually pre-booked a room online".
"Yes, I have a reservation under Protasov."
Say your surname twice, clearly.
"Of course. Here you are."
Handing your passport over — casual polite.
to check in / check-out
Standard check-out 12:00. Late check-out often costs extra.
a reservation
= a booking. Same thing.
a key card
Card that opens your room door — don't lose it.
"What's the Wi-Fi password, please?"
Ask them to write it if the number is long.
"Breakfast is included, from seven to ten."
"Included" or "extra". "Until 10 a.m." typical.
"Room 508, on the fifth floor. Enjoy your stay!"
Note down your room number in your phone.

08+

Listening · three short scenes

Play each recording once. Decide for each statement: True, False or Not stated. Then play it again and check.

Micro-theory · "Not stated" is a real answer. If the recording did not say it — do not guess. Not stated is not "wrong". It just means: I have no evidence. On exams and in real life, this is a strong skill.

Story 01 · Airport announcement

You are at Beijing Capital Airport. This announcement plays over the speakers.

1. The flight is going to Moscow.
2. Passengers should go to gate A17.
3. Passengers with children can board first.
4. There is free coffee for economy-class passengers.

Story 02 · The reservation problem

You are at the hotel reception. Something is wrong with your booking.

1. Your reservation is for tomorrow, not for today.
2. The available room is on the fifth floor.
3. The available room costs the same as the one you booked.
4. Breakfast is included in this cheaper room.
5. The available room is bigger than the one you booked.

Story 03 · Street directions

You are lost on a Beijing street. A local stops to help you.

1. You are looking for the subway.
2. You should walk straight for about two minutes.
3. The big sign is on your right.
4. The correct metro exit is exit C.
5. The metro station is called Beijing East.
09

Vocab · food, spicy & allergies

Chinese food is amazing — and often unexpectedly spicy. These phrases save you from tears at dinner.

Micro-theory · point + say. In small places the waiter brings a picture menu. Point and say "This one, please." Add "Not spicy, please." if you're unsure. That is a full order.
"Could I see the menu with pictures, please?"
Magic sentence in small restaurants.
"What do you recommend for a first-timer?"
People love this question. Great small talk too.
spicy · "Not spicy, please."
Chinese "a little spicy" is usually your "very spicy".
"I'm allergic to peanuts / seafood."
If serious — write it on your phone in Chinese too.
"Water, please. Not hot."
Default in China is hot water. Ask for cold or room-temperature.
"Could I have the bill, please?"
Or just make a small "writing" gesture in the air.
the bill / a receipt
Bill = what you pay. Receipt = the printed proof.
"WeChat, please."
Have a small amount of cash as backup.

11

Vocab · shopping & bargaining

Markets = bargain. Shopping malls = fixed price. Learn the difference or you'll look strange trying to haggle in H&M.

Micro-theory · the half-and-walk trick. Sellers at markets start high. A friendly reply is: "That's a bit expensive. Half price?" If they say no, smile, thank them, walk two steps away — they often call you back.
Ask
"How much is this one?"
Point clearly at the item.
Too much
"That's a bit expensive."
"Bit" softens it — sounds polite, not rude.
Counter
"Could you make it a bit cheaper? Half price?"
Half is a starting move. You'll meet in the middle.
Try
"Can I try it on?"
Clothes only. Point to yourself + the item.
Size
"Do you have a bigger / smaller size?"
Sizes in China run small. Ask up one.
Pay
"I'll take it. WeChat, please."
Decision + payment in one sentence.
No, thanks
"Thanks, I'll think about it."
Polite exit. Smile. Walk.
Receipt
"Could I have a receipt, please?"
In markets — often no. In shops — always.
12

Emergencies · pick the right phrase

Read the situation. Choose the sentence that fits best. Then say it aloud twice.

Golden rule for emergencies: short + slow + one clear noun. Panic voice = long tangled sentences. Calm voice = "I am lost. Metro?"

1. You get out of the metro and have no idea where the hotel is. You stop a young person. What do you say first?

2. Your stomach hurts badly. You are in the hotel lobby. What do you tell the receptionist?

3. You lose your passport. You need the embassy fast. What do you say to a policeman?

4. Someone bumps into you on the street. Nothing serious. What is the natural reply?

5. Your phone battery dies at 2 %. You need a socket at a café. What do you ask?

13

Cultural notes · do's & don'ts

Small habits that make a Russian teenager look either rude or friendly. Learn them once.

Do
Smile · nod · say "thank you" (谢谢, xièxie)
Even if the rest of the conversation is English. It opens doors.
Don't
Don't point with one finger
Point with an open hand or a slight nod.
Do
Use both hands to give / receive things
Money, cards, business cards, gifts.
Don't
Don't stick chopsticks straight up in rice
Looks like a funeral offering. Lay them flat on the rest.
Do
Download WeChat + Alipay + a translator
Baidu Maps or Amap for GPS. Google Maps often fails.
Don't
Don't rely on Instagram / Google — they are blocked
Get a VPN before you fly. After landing is too late.
Do
Carry your hotel card (with Chinese name) at all times
Show it to any taxi — instant "home".
Don't
Don't argue about the queue
Just insert yourself politely. It's normal.
14

Small talk · meeting locals

Six safe questions that work with students, waiters, taxi drivers who know some English. Practise each one aloud.

Starter 01
"Where are you from originally?"
Locals love this question. Follow up with the city name.
Starter 02
"What is a dish I really must try here?"
Instant local expert mode — people always have an answer.
Starter 03
"How do you say thank you in your language?"
You already know it, but ask anyway — it opens smiles.
Starter 04
"Is this your first time in this city too?"
Great with other tourists at a viewpoint.
Starter 05
"Do you play football / basketball / video games?"
Teenager to teenager, always works. Add a name of a player.
Starter 06
"How do I get to the metro from here?"
A safe fallback when you literally can't think of anything.
15

Mic drill · ten phrases without stopping

This is the anti-fear finisher. Say all ten phrases aloud, one after another, no pauses. If you get stuck — skip that one and go on. Do it three times.

Why: speaking without pausing to translate rewires the fear. After three passes, the phrases live in your mouth, not in your head. That is the real goal of today.
01
"Excuse me, do you speak English?"
Always ask. Some people do.
02
"The purpose of my visit is tourism."
Say it clean, no shrug.
03
"I'm staying at the Grand Beijing Hotel."
Hotel name loud + clear.
04
"To this address, please."
Golden taxi phrase.
05
"How much is this one?"
Point + ask.
06
"That's a bit expensive."
Soft, not rude.
07
"Not spicy, please. I'm allergic to peanuts."
Two facts, one sentence.
08
"Could I have the bill, please?"
"Could I" = polite.
09
"I'm lost. Could you help me?"
Never freeze — always ask.
10
"Thank you very much. Have a good day!"
Close every interaction like this.
