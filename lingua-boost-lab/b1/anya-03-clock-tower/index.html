Present Simple · everyday facts & habits

📘 MCQ · 6
First door on the Present floor — small and warm. Julia steps in. The room is full of ticking sounds. Every clock repeats the same second, over and over. This is where always, every day, usually live.

🌸 When to use

Facts, general truths, habits and routines. Repeated actions.

every dayusuallyon Mondaysalwaystwice a week

Form: I / you / we / they + V · he / she / it + V-s

She lives in Moscow and walks to school every morning.

Present Continuous · action right now / temporary

✏️ Type-in · 6
Second door. The floor here vibrates — everything is happening now. Julia sees people moving, painting, running mid-step.

🌸 When to use

Action happening at this moment · temporary situation · developing trend · fixed future arrangement.

nowright nowat the momentthis weekLook!

Form: am / is / are + V-ing

Look — it is raining. She is preparing for exams this month.

Present Perfect · connection past → now

🐛 Spot the error · 8
Third door. The clock here has two hands — one pointing at the past, one at the present. Both move together.

🌸 When to use

Past action with a result / connection to now · life experience (ever, never) · unfinished time (this week, today) · just / already / yet.

everneverjustalreadyyetsince 2020for 3 years

Form: have / has + V3 (past participle)

I have finished my homework. She has never seen the sea.

Present Perfect Continuous · duration up to now

🎯 Timeline sort · 10
Fourth door. A long river of time flows into the room and stops right at Julia's feet. Actions that started before — and are still flowing.

🌸 When to use

Action that started in the past and continues now — with emphasis on duration (how long) or recent activity visible now.

for 2 hourssince morningall daylatelyrecently

Form: have / has + been + V-ing

She has been studying for three hours. He has been running — look how tired he is.

Past Simple · one finished moment

🧩 Word order · 6
Julia walks down to the yellow floor. First past door. The clock here already stopped — every action inside is done, sealed, finished.

🟡 When to use

One finished action at a specific moment in the past.

yesterdaylast weektwo days agoin 2020when I was 10

Form: regular: V + -ed · irregular: V2 (went, saw, wrote)

Julia walked into the tower. She saw twelve clocks.

Past Continuous · action in progress in the past

🎴 Match · 8 while / when
Second yellow door. Two clocks tick side by side: one shows a moment (Past Simple), the other shows a long line (Past Continuous). They meet on the wall.

🟡 When to use

Action in progress at a moment in the past · long background action interrupted by a short one · two long parallel actions.

at 5 pm yesterdaywhilewhenall evening

Form: was / were + V-ing

While she was reading, the phone rang. At 8 pm we were having dinner.

Past Perfect · before another past action

✏️ Transform · 6
Third yellow door. Two suns rise over the past — one earlier, one later. Julia has to tell which is which.

🟡 When to use

An action that happened before another past action. The earliest of two past events.

by the timebeforeafteralready (in past)when she arrived, I …

Form: had + V3

By the time we arrived, the film had started. She had never seen snow before that trip.

Rewrite the two sentences as one, using Past Perfect for the earlier action.

8

Past Perfect Continuous · duration before another past

📘 MCQ · 6
Fourth yellow door. A long candle burns — it has been burning for hours before Julia walks in. That's the shape of this tense.

🟡 When to use

Action that was in progress for a duration up to a moment in the past — often to explain a past result.

for 2 hours (before …)since morning (that day)all afternoon before …

Form: had + been + V-ing

She had been studying for three hours when the lights went out. He was tired because he had been running.

Prediction (I think · probably) · promise · offer · instant decision (made right now).

tomorrownext weekI thinkprobablyI promise

Form: will + V (base) · negative won't

I think it will rain. I will help you — I promise. — Someone's at the door! — I 'll open it.

Future Continuous · in progress at a future moment

🐛 Spot & fix · 6
Third coral door. Julia sees herself tomorrow, at 5 pm — she is still walking through the tower. That's the picture Future Continuous paints.

🪸 When to use

An action in progress at a specific moment in the future · a fixed future arrangement.

at 5 pm tomorrowthis time next weekwhen you arrive

Form: will be + V-ing

At 8 pm tomorrow, I will be flying to London. This time next year, she will be studying at university.

Twelfth door. Highest room in the tower. The wall shows an arrow that ends before it should — every action here is finished by the arrow, not at.

An action that will be completed by a specific point in the future.

Form: will have + V3

By 2030, she will have finished university. By the time you arrive, we will have eaten.
