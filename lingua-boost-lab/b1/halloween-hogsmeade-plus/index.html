J0

The Halloween Quest · five stages, three storytellers, one potion

quest · intro
Welcome to the Halloween quest · click to enlarge Quest · Stage 0

Tonight you walk a short road. At the end of it, a cauldron is boiling in a cottage window on the edge of the woods, and the Halloween Potion is waiting to be brewed. But the potion has rules. Three storytellers will meet you before you reach it, and each one carries one piece of what you need to know.

  • Jack, the lantern-man, tells you where it all began — the night he outwitted the Devil and ended up walking the earth with a glowing turnip in his hand.
  • The Witch stirs the cauldron and shows you the seven ingredients, the order, the thirteen minutes. Miss one step and the potion turns to vinegar.
  • The Black Cat curls on the window ledge and whispers the small superstitions people still believe about her, about mirrors, about doorsteps, about sneezing on October 31.

By the end you will write your own Halloween potion — in English, with your own ingredients, your own glowing colour, your own warning. One role-play at the cauldron closes the night.

💡 Grammar you will meet along the way: Past Perfect in Jack's tale · imperatives + sequencers in the Witch's recipe · First Conditional + modals of possibility in the Cat's superstitions · narrative tenses in your own writing.

J1.4 Grammar focus · Past Perfect in narrative

Why "had learned", "had never been"?

In narrative English, when something happened BEFORE another past event, we use had + V3 (Past Perfect). The main story runs in Past Simple; everything that is already true at that point steps back into Past Perfect.

Sentence from the storyWhy Past Perfect?
The mice had learned to look elsewhere.They learned before the moment the Devil arrived.
Jack had never been one to pay for anything.A life-long truth, true before this night.
Hell, bound by its promise, refused him — the Devil had sworn never to claim him.The oath was made before Jack died.
1.When the sun rose, Jack realised the Devil ___ the coin inside a rock.
2.By the time the Devil climbed down, Jack ___ a cross into the bark.
3.Nobody in the village knew that Jack ___ the Devil twice.
4.Jack said he was ready — but he ___ three mugs of ale that night.

The Witch's Cauldron · seven ingredients, thirteen minutes

Quest · Stage 2 · Storyteller: the Witch

The Witch

She has been brewing in this cottage for four hundred and twelve years. Her recipe never changes, her patience is thin, and her cat has stronger opinions than she does. Listen carefully — she only says each step once.

The witch's cottage · bubbling green potion, jars, pumpkins, a watchful black cat · click to enlarge
🎬 Video · Witch's Brew Halloween Punch

J2.1 Reading · the recipe, in the witch's own voice

The old witch stirred the dark liquid in her black cauldron. "Come closer, dearie," she said, her voice like dry autumn leaves. "The potion needs seven ingredients, and if you forget even one, the night is spoiled. Listen carefully."

"First, pour three cups of moonlit water into the cauldron. Next, drop in five dried bat wings — carefully, dearie, they're brittle. Then, add one black feather from a crow that has flown over a graveyard. After that, sprinkle in a pinch of crushed spider's eggs. Now, stir anti-clockwise seven times — never the other way, or the potion will turn to vinegar. Finally, whisper the name of someone you miss, and seal the cauldron with a heavy iron lid. Boil for exactly thirteen minutes. The potion is ready when it glows green."

J2.2 Grammar · imperatives + sequencers

Recipes always speak in imperatives

A recipe tells you what to do. In English, the "do this" form is the bare verb — no subject, no auxiliary. Negative is Don't / Never + V.

FormExample
VPour three cups of water. Add one feather.
Don't + VDon't stir clockwise.
Never + VNever open the lid before thirteen minutes.

Sequencers glue the steps together in order:

PositionSequencers (B1-B2)
Opening stepFirst · First of all · To begin with
Middle stepsNext · Then · After that · Now · Once … , …
SimultaneousMeanwhile · At the same time · While …-ing
Final stepFinally · In the end · Last of all

J3.2 Reading · in the cat's own voice

A sleek black cat jumped onto the witch's shoulder, her eyes glowing like two small lanterns. "People say so many silly things about me," she purred.

"If a black cat crosses your path, they say, you will have bad luck — that's what English parents tell their children. But in Scotland, if a black cat appears on your doorstep, you'll become rich! If you hear a cat sneeze three times on Halloween night, you might catch a cold yourself. And if your cat refuses to look at the mirror on October 31st — well, it could mean she is seeing a ghost on the other side."

"Should you leave a saucer of cream on the step, she may bring you a quiet night. Unless you whistle indoors after midnight — then she will leave you to the ghosts."

"Me?" she yawned. "I just want cream. Humans. So dramatic."

J3.3 Grammar · First Conditional + modals of possibility

If + Present → will / might / may / could / should

The First Conditional talks about real, possible futures. The if-half uses Present Simple; the result-half uses a future form: will for certainty, might / may / could for possibility, should for prediction.

Modal in the resultHow certain?Example from the cat
will100% certainIf a black cat appears on your doorstep, you will become rich.
might / maymaybe (50%)She may bring you a quiet night.
couldpossible / theoreticalIt could mean she is seeing a ghost.
shouldexpected / likelyIf you leave cream, she should stay the night.

Unless = if not. Unless you whistle… = If you don't whistle…

J4

Write your own Halloween potion · 150-180 words

writing · recipe + narrative
Quest · Stage 4 · Your turn A ready potion · smoke curls up and smiles · click to enlarge

Task

Invent your own Halloween potion. It can be for love, for luck, for revenge, for passing an exam, for turning your little brother invisible for one afternoon — anything you like. Write 150-180 words. Your recipe must contain:

  1. Name of the potion and what it does. (1-2 sentences, present tense.)
  2. Five to seven ingredients (invented or borrowed from the witch).
  3. At least five sequencers — First, Next, Then, After that, Meanwhile, Finally.
  4. At least two imperatives in the negative — Don't / Never….
  5. One First Conditional warning using will / might / could — "If you add too much salt, the potion will…"

💡 Need a start? "GHOSTLIGHT POTION — brewed to help you fall asleep after a scary film." / "MIDNIGHT COURAGE — one sip and you can walk past the graveyard alone."

J5

Role-play · three voices at the cauldron

speaking · 3-way · 6 turns
Quest · Stage 5 · The three of you

Teacher plays the Witch and the Cat (switching voice). Student is You, the apprentice. Six turns total. Each ▶ reads a sample line.

Witch · W1 Welcome, dearie. Mind the smoke, and don't touch anything green. What have you brought me tonight?
You · T1 Greet the witch politely. Say what you've brought (one ingredient you invented in J4) and what the potion is for.
Cat · C1 Hmph. If you really want it to work, you'll have to tell her the order. And don't forget the bat wings.
You · T2 Explain the first three steps of your potion out loud, using First / Next / Then.
Witch · W2 Good. But what will happen if someone stirs it clockwise? Tell me — in English.
You · T3 Answer with a First Conditional warning. If someone stirs clockwise, the potion will / might / could…
Cat · C2 Smart child. Now, one last thing — ask the witch for something. Anything. The potion needs a wish inside it.
You · T4 Ask the witch a polite question / wish. Use Could you / Would you be able to / I was wondering if you might…
