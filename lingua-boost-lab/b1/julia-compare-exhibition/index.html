Scene 1 · At the gate · 🐘 🦒

It is Saturday. Julia, John and Emma are at the big summer Exhibition in the park. There are elephants, giraffes, peacocks and even a carousel. John runs to the first cage. "Wow, Julia, look at the elephant! It is much bigger than the lion." "Yes, it is," answers Julia. "And look up — the giraffe is the tallest animal in the whole zoo." "I can't see its head," says John. "That's because it's taller than two Dads together," laughs Julia.

Scene 2 · Peacock & carousel · 🦚 🎠

They walk past the birds. "Oh, look, Julia!" says Emma, "a peacock! It is more beautiful than any bird I know." But John doesn't care about birds. He pulls Emma's hand. "Come on, come on!" he shouts. "The carousel is the most interesting thing at the whole fair!"

Scene 3 · Mum & pancakes · 🥞

At twelve o'clock Mum comes with a basket. "Hungry, kids?" she asks. "I made pancakes with honey and raspberry jam." Emma takes a bite. "Mmm, Mum, your pancakes are better than the shop ones." John laughs. "And they are not as expensive as the shop pancakes!" Everyone smiles.

Scene 4 · Home with Barsik · 🐱

On the way home the sky gets darker and darker. When they open the door, Barsik is on the sofa, warm and lazy. "Hi, Barsik!" says Julia, "did you miss us?" Barsik yawns and purrs. He really thinks he is the best cat in the whole world.

Three families — the big picture

Theory · 1
Every adjective belongs to one of three families. Pick the family first → then use its ending.

★ The three families

FamilyComparative (than)Superlative (the …)
1 · short
1 syll + 2-syll in -y		big → bigger
tall → taller
happy → happier		the biggest
the tallest
the happiest
2 · long
2+ syll (beautiful · difficult · expensive · important)		more beautiful
more interesting		the most beautiful
the most interesting
3 · irregular
6 to memorise		good → better
bad → worse
far → farther / further
little → less
much / many → more
old → older / elder*		the best
the worst
the farthest / furthest
the least
the most
the oldest / eldest*

✗ No such forms: more big · gooder · beautifuler · interestinger · the goodest · the most beautifulest.

✓ Right: bigger · better · more beautiful · the best · the most interesting.

* elder / eldest only for family people (my elder sister). For things / age in general → older / oldest.

5

Spelling exceptions for short adjectives

Theory · 2
Four rules cover 99% of spelling traps when you add -er / -est.
5.1 · Silent -e → just -r / -st
BaseComparativeSuperlative
nicenicerthe nicest
largelargerthe largest
safesaferthe safest

✗ niceer · largeer   ✓ nicer · larger

5.2 · CVC → double the final consonant

CVC = consonant + vowel + consonant (one of each at the end). Double, then -er / -est.

Base (CVC)ComparativeSuperlative
bigbiggerthe biggest
hothotterthe hottest
thinthinnerthe thinnest
fatfatterthe fattest
Watch out ↳ if there are two vowels or two consonants, do NOT double.
clean (ea) → cleaner, cleanner
cold (ld) → colder, coldder
cheap → cheaper, cheapper
-w and -x are special: we never double them.
new → newer, newwer  ·  low → lower, lowwer
5.3 · -y after a consonant → -i
BaseComparativeSuperlative
happyhappierthe happiest
busybusierthe busiest
easyeasierthe easiest
prettyprettierthe prettiest
lazylazierthe laziest
heavyheavierthe heaviest

✗ happyer · more happy · the happyest   ✓ happier · the happiest

5.4 · Borderline 2-syll adjectives — either way

Both forms are correct for clever · narrow · simple · quiet · gentle · polite.

BaseShorter formLonger form
clevercleverermore clever
quietquietermore quiet
simplesimplermore simple
At B1 safe rule ↳ use the shorter form (cleverer, quieter, simpler).
6

Five patterns you need by B1

Theory · 3
Not just bigger than. B1 adds as…as · not as…as · less than · and · the more the better.
6.1 · Pattern 1 · Comparative + than

The elephant is bigger than the lion. · Mum's pancakes are better than the shop ones.

6.2 · Pattern 2 · the + superlative + in / of

The giraffe is the tallest animal in the zoo. · Barsik is the best cat in the whole world. · She is the youngest of three sisters.

Don't drop the: Giraffe is tallest animal → The giraffe is the tallest animal.
6.3 · Pattern 3 · as … as / not as … as / less … than

John is as fast as his sister. · The pigeon is not as beautiful as the peacock = The pigeon is less beautiful than the peacock.

Mini-trick ↳ not as X as ≡ less X than.
6.4 · Pattern 4 · Double comparative — and

The carousel went faster and faster. · Barsik is getting bigger and bigger. · The exhibition becomes more and more popular.

6.5 · Pattern 5 · the + comp, the + comp

The more you practise, the better you speak. · The hotter the day, the slower Barsik walks. · The older John gets, the more interesting his questions become.

7

B1+ · Modifiers & special structures

Theory · 4
At B1+ comparatives come with modifiers (how much bigger). Learn the strong / mid / weak set and three bonus structures.
7.1 · Intensifiers in front of the comparative
StrengthModifierExample
STRONG · big differencemuch · a lot · far · way (informal)The elephant is much bigger than the lion.
The giraffe is far taller than any animal.
Mum's pancakes are a lot better than the shop ones.
MID · clear differenceconsiderably · significantlyScottish Folds are considerably more expensive than ordinary cats.
WEAK · small differencea bit · a little · slightlyToday is a bit colder than yesterday.
Emma is slightly taller than Julia.
Don't use very, really, too with comparatives: very bigger · really taller · too more interesting — все NO. Use much, far, or a lot instead.
7.2 · by far + superlative — the champion

Put by far before the + superlative to say «гораздо самый / absolutely the best».

Barsik is by far the best cat in the world.
The carousel is by far the most exciting thing at the fair.
The giraffe is by far the tallest animal here.

7.3 · one of the + superlative + plural noun

When you're not sure it's number one, say it's one of them. Noun is always plural.

The peacock is one of the most beautiful birds I've ever seen.
Mum's pancakes are one of the best things on Sundays.
Emma is one of the funniest girls in the class.

Noun is plural even when meaning is singular: one of the most beautiful bird → one of the most beautiful birds.
7.4 · any & no + comparative

In negatives and questions:

  • any + comp — "at all / хоть сколько-то"   Can you walk any faster? · The carousel doesn't go any faster than this.
  • no + comp than — "не … чем / так же как"   Today is no colder than yesterday. · John is no taller than his sister.
7.5 · Short the + comp, the + comp without full clause

Fixed B1+ expressions where the second half is just a comparative with no subject/verb.

The sooner, the better. · The more, the merrier. · The bigger, the better. · The less, the worse.
