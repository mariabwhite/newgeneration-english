Mia's week without a school bell

My name is Mia, I'm 13, and I don't go to a regular school. I study at home with my mum and two online teachers, and honestly, I love it. People always ask me, «But don't you miss your friends?» The truth is — I see them more than before. I just see them when it's actually fun, not at 8 a.m. with sleepy faces in a classroom.

A normal day for me starts around 9. I have breakfast with my little brother, then I open my laptop and do maths and English until about 1 o'clock. After lunch I'm free. I draw, I take long walks with our dog Pepper, I help my mum cook. Twice a week I go to art class in the city, and on Fridays I meet my best friend Alina — we study Spanish together just for fun.

The best part isn't the free time, though. It's that when I don't understand something, I can stop and ask. No bell, no 45-minute race. Learning stops being scary. It becomes mine.

  • False — the text says the opposite.

Family trip to Lake Baikal

Last August my family and I took a four-day trip to Lake Baikal. My dad planned it as a surprise for my mum's birthday, so we only found out two days before we flew. From Moscow to Irkutsk it was a long five-hour flight — I slept most of it because we left at 6 in the morning.

We stayed in a small wooden guest house on Olkhon Island. There was no Wi-Fi, no TV, and almost no cars. At first I thought I'd be bored, but by the second day I didn't even want my phone. We walked along the lake every evening, and the sky turned pink around 9 o'clock. The water was so clear that you could see stones four or five metres down.

On the third day we took a boat to the north of the island. The driver, a quiet man called Sergey, told us stories about shamans and bears. He said that in winter the lake freezes so thick that cars can drive across it. My brother didn't believe him until Sergey showed us a photo on his phone.

On the last evening my mum cried a little. She said it was the best birthday of her life. We promised to come back next summer. I think we really will.

Speaking · describe a picture (OGE-style)Speaking

Look at the photo · follow the 7-point plan · 1-2 minutes
How to describe a photo: go in order. Don't jump around. 1 plan point = 1-2 sentences. End with your personal reaction.
Photo · family picnic in a sunny park
Your plan · 7 points
  1. Location — Where is the photo taken? (In the park / in the forest / by the lake…)
  2. People — Who do you see? (A family / parents + 2 kids…)
  3. Actions — What are they doing? (They are eating / sitting on a blanket…)
  4. Weather — What's the weather like? (It's sunny / warm / clear sky…)
  5. Time of year — What season is it? (I think it's summer because…)
  6. Mood — How do the people feel? (They look happy / relaxed…)
  7. Personal reaction — Do you like the photo? Why? (I like it because…)
💡 ⭐⭐A2Model answer · 7 sentences (tap if you need it)

In the photo I can see a family in a park. There are four people — mum, dad and two children. They are sitting on a red blanket and eating sandwiches. The weather looks sunny and warm, with a clear blue sky. I think it's late summer because the trees are still green. Everyone looks happy and relaxed. I really like this photo because it reminds me of family picnics with my own parents.

⭐⭐⭐B1B1 · level up — add interpretation

Once you've covered the 7 A2 points, add 3 more sentences interpreting the scene:

  • What do you think happened just before this photo was taken?
  • What might happen next?
  • What kind of family do you think this is? Why?
💡 B1 model paragraph

I think the family probably drove to this park early in the morning — I can see the picnic basket is still half full, so they haven't been there long. After lunch they'll probably play with the frisbee you can see in the air, and the dog will chase it. They look like a close, outdoorsy family who enjoy spending weekends in nature rather than at a shopping mall.

⭐⭐⭐⭐B2B2 · level up — personal opinion with hedging

Finish with 2 opinion sentences using hedging language (in my view, it seems to me, I'd argue) to show a nuanced take.

💡 B2 model paragraph

In my view, scenes like this are becoming rarer, because most teenagers today would rather stay at home and scroll on their phones than sit on a blanket for two hours. I'd argue, however, that photos like this one remind us why real family rituals — even boring ones — matter so much in the long run.

Speaking · mini roleplay · invite a friend to the cinemaSpeaking

3 turns · use the phrases in the box · teacher plays the friend
How to do a roleplay: imagine you're really inviting your friend. Answer in full sentences, not one word. If the friend says no — don't give up, suggest something else.

⭐⭐A2Scenario 1 · Invite a friend to the cinema

Turn 1 · YOU · invite
Invite your friend to the cinema. Say which film and when.
How about going to… Would you like to see… Let's go to the cinema on Saturday! They are showing… at 7 p.m.
Turn 2 · FRIEND (teacher) · objection
«Oh, I'd love to, but I'm busy on Saturday evening — I have a dentist appointment.»
Turn 2 · YOU · react
Show you understand your friend is busy. Say "that's a shame" and ask when they're free.
Oh, that's a shame! What a pity… When are you free? No worries, we can…
Turn 3 · YOU · suggest an alternative
Suggest a different day / different film / different time.
What if we go on Sunday? We could watch it on Sunday afternoon. How about Friday evening instead? Or we could just watch something at home.
💡 Model dialogue (tap if you need it)

You: Hey, how about going to the cinema on Saturday? They're showing the new Marvel film at 7 p.m.

Friend: Oh, I'd love to, but I'm busy on Saturday evening — I have a dentist appointment.

You: Oh, that's a shame! When are you free this weekend?

Friend: I'm free on Sunday after 2.

You: Perfect! What if we go on Sunday afternoon instead? There's a showing at 4 p.m.

⭐⭐⭐B1Scenario 2 · Return a faulty item to a shop (3 turns)

Turn 1 · YOU · explain the problem
Explain what you bought, when, and what's wrong. Use should have or would like to.
I bought this last week and… It should have worked, but… I'd like to return it, please.
Turn 2 · SHOP ASSISTANT (teacher)
«I'm afraid we can only offer a shop credit, not a cash refund. Would that work for you?»
Turn 3 · YOU · ask for an alternative politely
Say you'd prefer a refund and ask if a manager could help.
Could you check with your manager? I'd really prefer a refund if possible. Is there any way we could…
💡 B1 model dialogue

You: Hi, I bought these headphones last week, but one side has already stopped working. I'd like to return them, please.

Assistant: I'm afraid we can only offer a shop credit, not a cash refund. Would that work for you?

You: I'd really prefer a refund if possible, since they broke so quickly. Could you check with your manager?

⭐⭐⭐⭐B2Scenario 3 · Negotiate a schedule change with a teacher (4 turns)

Turn 1 · YOU · open with a hedged request
Ask politely to move your lesson. Use hedging: I was wondering, would it be possible, if you don't mind…
I was wondering if we could… Would it be at all possible to… I hope this isn't inconvenient.
Turn 2 · TEACHER (teacher)
«Hmm, Thursday evening is already fully booked. I'm not sure I can fit you in.»
Turn 3 · YOU · reason + offer alternatives
Give a short reason (hospital visit / family event) and offer two alternative slots.
The reason is that… I could do Wednesday evening or Friday morning instead. Whichever works best for you.
Turn 4 · YOU · confirm + thank
Agree to one option, confirm the time, thank politely.
That sounds perfect — thank you so much. I really appreciate your flexibility. See you on Wednesday at 6 p.m. then.
💡 B2 model dialogue

You: I was wondering if we could possibly move my Thursday lesson. I hope this isn't too inconvenient.

Teacher: Hmm, Thursday evening is already fully booked. I'm not sure I can fit you in.

You: The reason is that I have a family event. I could do Wednesday evening or Friday morning instead — whichever works best for you.

Teacher: Wednesday at 6 p.m. would actually work.

You: That sounds perfect — thank you so much. I really appreciate your flexibility. See you on Wednesday then.
