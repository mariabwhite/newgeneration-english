01

What formal register actually is

Not «harder words». Register is the whole voice of a piece — vocabulary, grammar, tone, and how visible you are behind the sentence. Same message, three registers:

Informal

Hey — sorry about the wait! Fix is coming, promise. Cheers, M.

Neutral

Hi Anna — apologies for the delay. We'll have this fixed by Friday. Thanks for your patience. — Maria

Formal

Dear Ms Ivanova, I am writing to apologise for the delay in resolving your case. I can confirm that a full solution will be in place no later than Friday, 5 August. Yours sincerely, M. Burtseva

Six markers a grader scans for in the first sentence

MarkerInformal → Formal
SalutationHi → Dear + name / Dear Sir or Madam
Contractionsdon't · won't · I'm → do not · will not · I am
Phrasal verbsput up with → tolerate · find out → discover · look into → investigate
Personal pronouns«I» heavy → passive / impersonal («It is felt that…»)
Vague fillersa lot of / stuff / things → considerable / matters / issues
Sign-offCheers / Take care → Yours sincerely (name known) / Yours faithfully (Sir/Madam)

The «Yours» rule Olympiad graders check first

Yours sincerely — when you opened with a name (Dear Ms Ivanova).
Yours faithfully — when you opened without a name (Dear Sir or Madam).
Mismatched salutation and sign-off is the single most common reason a letter loses the «format» band.

02

Eight formal letter types — one page reference

Every Olympiad and CAE letter task is a variant of one of these eight. Each tile shows purpose, canonical opening, and canonical closing. Memorise the openings — graders reward the ones marked italic.

1 · Application

Applying for a job, course, scholarship, programme.
I am writing to apply for the position of… With reference to your advertisement in… I look forward to hearing from you. / Yours faithfully,

2 · Cover letter

Sent with a CV; frames the CV for a specific reader.
Please find enclosed my CV in support of my application for… I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my application further.

3 · Complaint

Reporting a fault, poor service, unfair treatment.
I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with… I wish to draw your attention to… I trust you will take immediate action. / I look forward to your prompt reply.

4 · Enquiry

Asking a company / institution for information.
I am writing to enquire about… I would be grateful if you could send me further details concerning… I look forward to receiving your reply.

5 · Request

Asking for a favour, permission, or specific action.
I am writing to request… I would be most grateful if you could… Thank you in advance for your assistance.

6 · Apology

Formal acknowledgement of a mistake or delay.
I am writing to apologise for… Please accept my sincere apologies for… I hope this incident will not affect our future relations.

7 · Recommendation

Endorsing a person, product, or place.
I am writing to recommend Ms X for… It is my pleasure to endorse… I would recommend her without reservation.

8 · Invitation / Reply

Formal invitation to an event, or formal RSVP.
We would be honoured if you could attend… I am delighted to accept your invitation to… We look forward to your presence. / Yours sincerely,

Signposting bank — glue for the middle paragraphs

FunctionFormal phrases
AddingFurthermore, · Moreover, · In addition, · What is more,
ContrastingHowever, · Nevertheless, · That said, · On the other hand,
Giving examplesFor instance, · A case in point is… · To illustrate,
ConcludingTo sum up, · In conclusion, · All things considered,
Softening a requestI would appreciate it if you could… · Could you possibly… · I wonder whether it might be possible to…

Grade 5 / 5 Application Tour Guide · Hannah Johnson
Task prompt
You see this advert in a newspaper: «Tour guides needed. We need a hard-working team of people to work as tour guides throughout the summer period. A good knowledge of your local area is required, as well as the ability to get on well with people. If you are at least 18 years old, write to us and tell us about your character and interests. We would also like you to say what you think is one of the highlights of your area and why.» Write a letter applying for the job advertised.
Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing in response to the advert I saw in the newspaper seeking people to work as tour guides. I think I would make a very good tour guide and I wish to apply for the job. There are a number of reasons that I think I would make an ideal employee for you. I am nineteen years old and have just completed my first year studying archaeology at the university. Although I did not grow up in this city, I really love it here and have enjoyed getting to know the city. Now, I know it like the back of my hand, but I still have the enthusiasm of a visitor. For this reason, I think I would be very good at showing other visitors around. I have always been sociable and I like meeting and talking with people; for example, when I started university I joined five different clubs! That was a bit much, so now I choose my three favourite which I'm still actively involved in. I believe the highlight of our area is the history. It is a very great history and is known extensively. There are many archaeological sites, which are well shown, for people to see and visit and understand. Not many cities have the opportunity to educate visitors so much about the past. I appreciate your attention to my letter and I hope you will consider my application. If you need any more information please feel free to contact me. I hope to hear from you soon. Yours faithfully, Hannah Johnson

What this Grade-5 letter does right

Opening. Salutation + purpose statement in one sentence — «I am writing in response to the advert…». Structure. Three body paragraphs, one per element of the task (why suitable · about me · highlight of area). Register. No contractions except one deliberate softening («I'm still actively involved»). Sign-off. Matches «Dear Sir or Madam» → Yours faithfully. Perfect pair.

Have you got the right personality to work in a team? Tell us why you want to participate and why you should be chosen. Write your letter of application.

It is obvious that my physical skills and my body strength are bound to help me to survive under difficult circumstances during my stay on a remote island. As far as my personality is concerned I do believe that I am very polite and honest with people. I have great communicational skills and this derives from the fact that I am used to be a member of different sports groups. My experience in climbing mountain Olympus has supplied me with a great amount of patience. I am always willing to offer my help and experience to less-experienced team-mates and to give any piece of advice, should it be important. I strongly believe that I am the perfect candidate for this entertaining television programme. I really indulge myself in being under difficult conditions supplied only with the basic objects. It seems to me that it is an outlet for my energy. I enclose my curriculum vitae and photographs. I would like to thank you in advance for considering my application. Grade 5 / 5 Complaint Wrongful accusation · Michael Mathers
Task prompt
You recently had an unpleasant experience when you were shopping in a department store. One of the assistants wrongly accused you of shoplifting. Although you were able to prove that you had paid for the item in question, you received no apology. Write a letter to the manager of the shop, saying why you are angry and disappointed and asking for some kind of compensation for the way you were treated. Say that you will take further action if you do not receive an official apology.
Dear Mr Menton, I am writing about a recent incident in your shop in which I had the misfortune to be involved. The incident I refer to is when one of your staff stopped me and accused me of shoplifting in front of about 20 other people. Needless to say, this was an extremely embarrassing situation for me. Thank goodness I had kept the receipt to prove that I had paid for everything on my person at the time, but to be accused of stealing like that in front of all those people — some of whom I knew personally — was very humiliating and degrading. The shop assistant used a very accusatory tone… But the thing that angered and dismayed me most was not the accusation itself — after all, misunderstandings happen sometimes — but the accuser's failure to acknowledge his mistake or apologise to me. When I showed him the receipt, he simply walked off mumbling about how 'shady' a character I looked. Considering how much embarrassment I was caused, I do not think it was too much to have expected a simple apology either, do you? Which brings me back to why I am writing; if I cannot get an apology from this assistant, then I would like a formal one from you instead. If an official apology is not forthcoming and I am not compensated in some way — with a shopping voucher for example — then be warned that I will take further steps to ensure that I get justice. I look forward to hearing from you at the very earliest convenience. Yours sincerely, Michael Mathers

What this Grade-5 complaint gets right

Named recipient → sign-off Yours sincerely. Perfect pair. Structure: incident → what specifically angered you → what you now want → warning. Register control: strong feeling without losing formality — «Needless to say», «Thank goodness», «Considering how much embarrassment I was caused». Escalation phrase: «I will take further steps to ensure that I get justice» — direct but non-threatening. Note the passive: «I was caused», «I am not compensated» — passive is the standard register-lift in complaint letters.

04

Passive voice — the register-lifter

Formal writing hides the actor. Complaint letters, official notices, and academic writing use passive to shift focus from who did it to what was done. Twelve tenses, one formula: be + past participle.

TenseActivePassive
Present simpleThey deliver the mail at nine.The mail is delivered at nine.
Present continuousThey are repairing the road.The road is being repaired.
Present perfectThey have cancelled the flight.The flight has been cancelled.
Past simpleThey stopped him.He was stopped.
Past continuousThey were watching us.We were being watched.
Past perfectThey had lost the file.The file had been lost.
Future simpleThey will send the report.The report will be sent.
ModalThey must review the case.The case must be reviewed.
Modal perfectThey should have told us.We should have been told.
GerundI don't like people ignoring me.I don't like being ignored.
InfinitiveHe expects them to invite him.He expects to be invited.
Perfect infinitiveShe is glad they chose her.She is glad to have been chosen.

  2. By the time we arrived, someone had already opened the envelope.
  3. They are still investigating the incident.
  4. The team should have documented every blink.
  5. Reporters were following her for weeks.
  6. Nobody has asked the patient for his opinion.
Show keys
  1. The winners will be announced on Friday.
  2. By the time we arrived, the envelope had already been opened.
  3. The incident is still being investigated.
  4. Every blink should have been documented.
  5. She was being followed by reporters for weeks.
  6. The patient has not been asked for his opinion.
Drill 2 · Fill in the gap with the correct passive form of the verb in brackets · tense hint given

  2. Your application   (process · present perfect) and a decision will follow shortly.
  3. The new stadium   (build · present continuous) at the moment.
  4. Nothing   (do · past simple) about the complaint at the time.
  5. By 2030, the treatment   (approve · future perfect) in every EU state.
  6. These files must   (destroy · modal) after seven years.

  2. Your application has been processed and a decision will follow shortly.
  3. The new stadium is being built at the moment.
  4. Nothing was done about the complaint at the time.
  5. By 2030, the treatment will have been approved in every EU state.
  6. These files must be destroyed after seven years.
Drill 3 · Spot the error · one word/phrase is wrong in each passive sentence — fix it
  1. The letter was written from the manager himself.
  2. My phone is stolen last night at the concert.
  3. She said the report was writing by an intern.
  4. All the questions have been answering during the interview.
  5. The problem should be solve before the end of the week.
  6. The winner is been announced at 8 pm.
Show keys
  1. «from» → by — agent introduced with by, not from.
  2. «is» → was — past time reference «last night» → past simple passive.
  3. «writing» → written — passive needs past participle, not -ing.
  4. «have been answering» → have been answered — same error, wrong participle.
  5. «be solve» → be solved — modal + be + past participle.
  6. «is been» → is being (or will be) — «is been» is not a valid form.
05

Passive report — the academic distancing move

The passive-report construction (subject + be + reporting verb + to-infinitive) hides the source of a claim. Journalists, scientists and diplomats use it constantly: It is thought that… / He is believed to be…

Two parallel patterns — same meaning

Pattern A · Impersonal. It + is/was + reporting verb + that + full clause.
It is believed that Theo was unable to think.

Pattern B · Personal. Subject + is/was + reporting verb + to-infinitive.
Theo is believed to have been unable to think.

Tense rule. If the reported action is at the same time as the reporting → to + infinitive. If earlier → to have + past participle.

Reporting verbs that take this construction

believe · say · think · know · claim · report · consider · rumour · allege · estimate · expect · understand

Drill 1 · Key-word transformation · rewrite using the CAPITAL word · 2–5 words
  1. People believed that Theo could no longer think. BELIEVED
    Theo   think.
  2. The lab reported that Dr Reyes would publish the following week. ANNOUNCED
    Dr Reyes   the following week.
  3. Everyone knew Naomi had hidden the family connection. KNOWN
    Naomi   the family connection.
  4. The press claims that the McGill group is re-analysing the data. CLAIMED
    The McGill group   the data.
  5. Sources say Theo blinked one word at three in the morning. SAID
    Theo   one word at three in the morning.
  6. Colleagues estimate that eleven weeks passed before anyone realised. ESTIMATED
    Eleven weeks   before anyone realised.
Show keys
  1. Theo was believed to no longer be able to think. (same-time report → to + inf; here «be able to think»)
  2. Dr Reyes announced (that) she would publish the following week. (reported speech; backshift + «the following week»)
  3. Naomi was known to have hidden the family connection. (earlier action → to have + past participle)
  4. The McGill group is claimed to be re-analysing the data. (same-time, continuous → to be + -ing)
  5. Theo is said to have blinked one word at three in the morning. (earlier action)
  6. Eleven weeks are estimated to have passed before anyone realised.
Drill 2 · Rewrite Pattern A (impersonal, «It is…») ↔ Pattern B (personal, subject + is/was)
  1. It is believed that she has left the country. → (Pattern B, start with «She…»)
  2. The prime minister is said to be considering resignation. → (Pattern A, start with «It…»)
  3. It was reported that the two companies had signed a deal. → (Pattern B, start with «The two companies…»)
  4. Ancient texts are known to have described the same eclipse. → (Pattern A, start with «It…»)
  5. It is expected that the results will be published next month. → (Pattern B, start with «The results…»)
  6. The witness is thought to have seen the entire incident. → (Pattern A, start with «It…»)
Show keys
  1. She is believed to have left the country. (has left → to have left; earlier action)
  2. It is said that the prime minister is considering resignation.
  3. The two companies were reported to have signed a deal.
  4. It is known that ancient texts described the same eclipse.
  5. The results are expected to be published next month. (future → to be + past participle)
  6. It is thought that the witness saw the entire incident.
Drill 3 · Same-time or earlier? Choose the correct infinitive form
  1. The suspect is believed (to hide / to have hidden) the evidence before the police arrived.
  2. The comet is expected (to return / to have returned) in 2061.
  3. Recent studies are said (to confirm / to have confirmed) the earlier findings — the papers came out last year.
  4. The star is reported (to be / to have been) the largest ever observed — the observation is happening now.
  5. By the time they landed, the astronauts were known (to lose / to have lost) radio contact for over an hour.
  6. Dr Reyes is understood (to prepare / to have prepared) a further statement for tomorrow.
Show keys
  1. to have hidden — «before the police arrived» → earlier than the reporting.
  2. to return — future event from the reporting moment → simple to-inf.
  3. to have confirmed — «came out last year» → earlier.
  4. to be — «happening now» → same-time.
  5. to have lost — «by the time they landed» → earlier than the past reference.
  6. to prepare — action planned for tomorrow (future to reporter) → simple to-inf.
06

Modal perfects · needn't / didn't need · wish

Three grammar rooms Olympiad writing constantly tests. Get the meaning gap right — graders mark it as «Language range».

FormMeaningVoyager-flavoured example
must have + PPCertain conclusion about the past.Theo must have recognised her handwriting.
can't / couldn't have + PPCertain that something did NOT happen.Naomi can't have been more than three when he left.
might / may / could have + PPPossible in the past (we don't know).He might have blinked the name earlier and no-one saw.
should have + PPRight thing wasn't done (regret / criticism).She should have told the lab from day one.
needn't have + PPSomething was done — but it wasn't necessary.She needn't have hidden her surname. (She did — pointlessly.)
didn't need to + infNot necessary — and we don't know / it wasn't done.She didn't need to inform the press. (Neutral — maybe she did, maybe she didn't.)

The «needn't vs didn't need» trap

needn't have + PP = done, but pointless.
didn't need to + inf = not necessary; often not done.
Olympiad key-word tests love this pair. If the sentence signals the action happened → needn't have.

Wish + past perfect — the regret construction

wish + past simple — regret about now. I wish I knew the answer.
wish + past perfect — regret about the past. I wish I had told her sooner.
wish + would + inf — annoyance / desire for change. I wish he would stop lying.

Drill 1 · Key-word transformation · 2–5 words
  1. It was not necessary for Naomi to inform the press. NEED
    Naomi   inform the press.
  2. Naomi told her team late — she regrets it. WISHES
    She   her team sooner.
  3. It was a mistake for the lab not to record every blink. SHOULD
    The lab   every blink.
  4. Naomi hid the connection — but it turned out to be pointless. NEEDN'T
    Naomi   the connection.
  5. I'm certain Theo recognised her voice. MUST
    Theo   her voice.
  6. Perhaps he blinked earlier and nobody saw. MIGHT
    He   earlier and nobody saw.
Show keys
  1. Naomi did not need to / needn't inform the press. (neutral — action not confirmed)
  2. She wishes she had told her team sooner. (wish + past perfect for past regret)
  3. The lab should have recorded every blink.
  4. Naomi needn't have hidden the connection. (action happened, was unnecessary)
  5. Theo must have recognised her voice.
  6. He might have blinked earlier and nobody saw.
Drill 2 · «needn't have» vs «didn't need to» · pick the correct one
  1. I (needn't have brought / didn't need to bring) my umbrella — it wasn't raining, so I left it at home.
  2. I (needn't have brought / didn't need to bring) my umbrella — turns out the whole day was sunny and I carried it for nothing.
  3. You (needn't have paid / didn't need to pay) the bill — she'd already covered it, but you didn't know.
  4. She (needn't have worried / didn't need to worry) about the interview — she was going to be offered the job regardless.
  5. We (needn't have booked / didn't need to book) a table — the restaurant was empty, so we walked in.
  6. You (needn't have cooked / didn't need to cook) — we're going out. Stop, put the pan down!
Show keys
  1. didn't need to bring — not necessary AND not done («I left it at home»).
  2. needn't have brought — done, but pointless («I carried it for nothing»).
  3. needn't have paid — you paid, then found out it wasn't necessary.
  4. needn't have worried — she did worry, uselessly.
  5. didn't need to book — didn't book, wasn't necessary.
  6. needn't have cooked — cooking is happening now, mid-action, and it's unnecessary.
Drill 3 · Wish transformation · rewrite each regret using WISH
  1. I am sorry I don't know Japanese. → I wish  
  2. Naomi regrets not telling her lab from day one. → She wishes  
  3. It's annoying — my brother won't stop borrowing my laptop. → I wish  
  4. I regret buying that ticket last month. → I wish  
  5. Ivan is sorry he doesn't have more free time this term. → He wishes

  1. I wish I knew Japanese. (regret about now → past simple)
  2. She wishes she had told her lab from day one. (past regret → past perfect)
  3. I wish my brother would stop borrowing my laptop. (annoyance → would + inf)
  4. I wish I hadn't bought that ticket. (past regret → past perfect negative)

07

Inversion — three constructions Olympiad graders test

Inversion means reversing subject and auxiliary. It sounds formal and «written» — perfect for the last paragraph of a complaint or a persuasive opening. Cambridge / British Council split it into three rooms.

The core pattern. Trigger phrase (front of sentence) + auxiliary + subject + main verb.
If there is no auxiliary in the original, add do / does / did. Rarely do we find such talent.

Construction 1 · Conditional inversion (drops the «if»)

TypeWith «if»Inverted (formal)
1st conditionalIf you should require further details…Should you require further details…
2nd conditionalIf we were to ask the right question…Were we to ask the right question…
3rd conditionalIf we had asked the right question, we would have caught the signal.Had we asked the right question, we would have caught the signal.

Register. «Should you require…» is the standard formal closing offer in application/enquiry letters. Ivan — use this in Section 8's mock task.

Construction 2 · Negative-adverbial inversion

When you front any of these adverbs / adverbial phrases, inversion follows.

TriggerFull example (from British Council C1 grammar bank)
NeverNever had they seen so many people in the village.
Rarely / SeldomRarely do we find such talent. · Seldom has she taken a day off work.
LittleLittle did she know then that she would become the company director.
Not only … but alsoNot only did he win the match but he came back from a difficult start.
Under no circumstancesUnder no circumstances should children travel without an adult.
At no time · In no way · On no accountIn no way did we agree to this.
Only after · Only when · Only then · Only laterOnly after eleven weeks did the team realise the truth. · Only later did they discover they hadn't been told the truth.

Construction 3 · Hardly / Scarcely / Barely + when · No sooner + than

Two events, one quickly after the other. Use past perfect in the inverted clause.

PatternExample
Hardly had X … when YHardly had we sat down when we were told to evacuate the building.
Scarcely had X … when YScarcely had the votes been counted when the new president was pushed in front of TV cameras.
Barely had X … when YBarely had she got back in bed when her alarm went off.
No sooner had X … than YNo sooner had the game started than the captain was taken ill.
