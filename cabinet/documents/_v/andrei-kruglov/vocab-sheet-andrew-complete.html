B · Science & research 16 items

Источник: science-systems-c1-andrew · science-systems-c1-andrew-toefl labs.

#TermPOS / fieldMeaning + example
1cognitionnoun · psychologyMental processes — perception, memory, reasoning. Chronic sleep deprivation measurably impairs cognition.
2neuroplasticitynoun · neuroscienceThe brain's capacity to rewire itself in response to experience. Adult neuroplasticity underpins post-stroke recovery.
3peer reviewnoun · academic processPre-publication evaluation by independent experts. Without peer review, a paper carries little scientific weight.
4hypothesisnoun · researchA testable statement proposed as the starting point of inquiry. Our working hypothesis is that early exposure accelerates fluency.
5replicationnoun · researchReproducing a study to confirm its findings. The replication crisis has reshaped how psychology is published.
6statistical significancenoun · statisticsA result unlikely to be due to chance (usually p < 0.05). The effect reached statistical significance only in the older cohort.
7confirmation biasnoun · cognitive psychTendency to notice evidence that supports what you already believe. Peer review is designed in part to counteract confirmation bias.
8CRISPRnoun · biotechPrecise gene-editing technology using guide RNA and Cas9. CRISPR trials for sickle-cell disease began in 2019.
9heritabilitynoun · geneticsThe proportion of trait variation attributable to genetics. The heritability of height exceeds 80 per cent.
10machine learningnoun · computer sciSystems that improve performance from data rather than explicit rules. Machine learning now diagnoses certain cancers more accurately than specialists.
11neural networknoun · computer sciLayered mathematical model loosely inspired by biological neurons. Deep neural networks underpin modern language translation.
12quantum entanglementnoun · physicsA correlated state in which measurement of one particle instantly determines the state of another. Quantum entanglement is the resource behind emerging quantum networks.
13exoplanetnoun · astronomyA planet orbiting a star outside our solar system. Over five thousand exoplanets have been catalogued to date.
14radiocarbon datingnoun · archaeologyAge estimation of organic material via the decay of carbon-14. Radiocarbon dating places the artefact in the fourth century BCE.
15biocompatibleadj · materialsNot harmful to living tissue — safe for implants. The new polymer is fully biocompatible and resorbs within six months.
16superconductornoun · physicsMaterial that conducts electricity with zero resistance below a critical temperature. High-temperature superconductors remain the holy grail of grid engineering.

C · Renewable energy & climate tech 12 items

Источник: toefl-2026-lsw-andrew lab (новый урок 30.09–01.10.2026).

#TermPOSMeaning + example
1exponentialadjGrowing at an accelerating rate rather than a steady one. Battery-storage deployment rose exponentially in 2025.
2tandemadj / nounA two-layer solar cell stacking two absorber materials. Silicon–perovskite tandems now exceed 34 % efficiency.
3degradationnounLoss of performance over time in a device or material. Rapid degradation was long the Achilles heel of perovskite cells.
4monetizeverbTo turn something into a stream of revenue. Occidental monetizes captured CO₂ through enhanced oil recovery.
5undermineverbTo weaken something indirectly and gradually. Critics argue that enhanced oil recovery undermines the climate rationale for direct air capture.
6deploymentnounInstallation and putting into service at scale. Utility-scale storage deployment reached 108 GW in 2025.
7rampverbTo scale production upwards, often steeply. Perovskite manufacturing must ramp to gigawatt scale to move the needle.
8thresholdnounA critical crossover point at which behaviour changes. Perovskite tandems appear to have crossed the commercial threshold in 2024.
9aspirationaladjAmbitious but not yet guaranteed to be reached. The $100-per-tonne target for DAC is aspirational, not committed.
10viabilitynounThe ability to work under real-world commercial conditions. The long-term viability of the sector still rests on further cost reductions.
11order of magnitudenoun phraseA factor of roughly ten times up or down. Current DAC costs remain an order of magnitude above the aspirational target.
12Achilles heelnoun · idiomA single critical weakness that undermines an otherwise strong system. Stability was long treated as perovskite's Achilles heel.
