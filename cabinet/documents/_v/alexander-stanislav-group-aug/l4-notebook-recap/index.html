Some / Any / something / anything / nothing

One rule. some / something — for «yes» sentences and polite offers. any / anything — for questions and «no» sentences. nothing — keep the verb positive.
some
affirmation · polite request
I have some free time. · Would you like some tea?
any
question · negation
Do you have any tea? · I don't have any tea.
something
"a thing" · affirmation
I want something to eat.
anything
question · negation
Do you need anything? · I don't need anything.
nothing
"no thing" · with an affirmative verb
I know nothing. · There is nothing new.
⚠ No double «no» in English. With nothing the verb is positive: I know nothing ✓. I don't know nothing ❌ → I don't know anything ✓.
1. I have ? free time today.
Affirmation → some.
2. Do you have ? plans for tonight?
Question → any.
3. I don't have ? colleagues in Berlin.
Negation → any.
4. I want to tell you ? about my week.
Affirmation → something.
5. Would you like ? coffee?
Polite request → some.
6. There is ? new today.
"Nothing new" is a fixed expression.
2

Quantifiers · a few / few / several · a little / little

One question: can you count it — 1, 2, 3? Yes → a few / few / several. No (water, time, money, coffee, sleep) → a little / little.
a few
countable · + enough
I have a few colleagues in Berlin.
few
countable · − not enough
I have few free days this month.
several
countable · neutral
Several managers came to the training.
a little
uncountable · + enough
I have a little free time.
little
uncountable · − not enough
I have little time before the call.
💡 Trick: a before few / little = "some, enough". No a = "very little, not enough".
1. I have ? colleagues in the Berlin office.
colleagues — countable → a few.
2. Would you like ? water?
water — uncountable → a little.
3. We have ? time — hurry.
time — uncountable, not enough → little.
4. ? managers came to the training.
managers — countable → Several.
5. She speaks ? foreign languages.
languages — countable, enough → a few.
3

Articles a / an · chosen by sound, not by letter

a — before a consonant SOUND. an — before a vowel SOUND. Traps: an hour (h is silent), a university (sounds like [ju]), an MP3 (starts with «em»), a European (starts with [j]).
a · consonant sound
    an · vowel sound
      university hour European client MP3 one-time offer honest man CEO old friend pencil apple large office interview
      💡 CEO sounds like «si-i-ou» → starts with [s] → a CEO. MP3 sounds like «em-pi-three» → starts with [e] → an MP3.

      5

      Prepositions of time · AT / ON / IN

      Trick: a clock time → AT. a day or a date → ON. a long period → IN. Three exceptions: at night, at lunchtime, at the weekend (UK).
      AT · exact moment
        ON · day / date
          IN · longer period
            7 o'clock noon midnight night lunchtime the moment Monday 16 May Friday morning my birthday Christmas Day July 2026 winter the morning an hour
            ⚠ 12 pm = noon (12:00 midday). 12 am = midnight (00:00). a.m. = before noon. p.m. = after noon.

            Comparatives · irregular: good / bad / much / little / far

            Five words that DON'T follow the +er / +est rule. Learn them by heart.
            BaseComparativeSuperlativeMeaning
            goodbetterthe bestgood → better → the best of all
            badworsethe worstbad → worse → the worst of all
            much / manymorethe mostmuch / many → more → the most
            littlelessthe leastlittle → less → the least
            farfurther / fartherthe furthest / farthestfar → further/farther → the furthest/farthest
            💡 Rule: 1 syllable → +er / +est (fast → faster → fastest). 2+ syllables → more / the most (expensive → more expensive). After a comparative, use than.
            1. Today the weather is ? than yesterday.
            good → better (irregular).
            2. This is ? restaurant in town.
            good → the best.
            3. My headache is ? today.
            bad → worse.
            4. This is ? traffic jam of the year.
            bad → the worst.
            5. I need ? time to finish the report.
            much → more.
            7

            Adverbs of frequency · 9 words on the scale 100 % → 0 %

            Where to put it: BEFORE a normal verb (I always drink coffee). AFTER the verb be (She is never late). That's the only rule.
            1. Correct: I ? in the evening.
            Normal verb → adverb BEFORE it.
            2. Correct: She ? late.
            After be → adverb AFTER it.
            3. Correct: We ? the client on Fridays.
            Normal verb → adverb before it.

            • Could you help me out? — polite request for help
            • Do you mind if I …? — polite permission-seeking

            • Thanks anyway. — polite thanks when help is declined
            16–20 Clarification & understanding
            • Sorry, I didn't catch that. — polite «I didn't hear you»
            • What do you mean? — asking for clarification
            • I'm not sure I follow. — softly signalling confusion
            • Could you say that again? — polite request for repetition
            • Oh, got it! — «understood!»
            21–25 Agreement / disagreement
            • Exactly! — emphatic agreement
            • Fair enough. — reasonable acceptance
            • I see your point. — acknowledging even in disagreement
            • I'm not sure about that. — polite doubt
            • You might be right. — partial concession
            26–30 Everyday actions
            • I'm on my way. — arriving shortly
            • I just woke up. — freshly awake
            • I'm heading home. — leaving for home
            • Let's grab some coffee. — casual invitation for coffee
            • I'll be right back. — brief absence — returning soon
            31 Planning & meetings

            • Anytime after 3 p.m. works for me. — offering a time window

            ⚡ Confused pairs · one glance, one rule

            other
            другие (много) · plural
            the other countries · other managers
            another
            другой (один) · singular
            another country · another meeting
            a lot / many
            много (считаемое)
            a lot of / many meetings · many people
            much
            много (несчитаемое)
            how much time · not much water
            spend time
            проводить время (нейтральное)
            I spend a lot of time at work.
            have time in a hotel
            предпочтительнее в этом контексте
            I'd love to have some time in a nice hotel.
            close
            закрывать / близкий
            Please close the door. · a close friend.
            clothes
            одежда · plural noun
            official clothes for the meeting.
            closet
            шкаф / туалет · закрытое пространство
            Hang your suit in the closet.
            solve
            решать (проблему) — быстрый ответ
            I need to solve many issues at work.
            resolve
            разрешить (конфликт) — глубже
            We resolved the dispute peacefully.

            решить (выбор из вариантов)
            I decided to take the offer.

            🗣️ 8 Wh-questions · daily-routine drill

            1. ___ do you do?
            «What do you do?» = «Кем ты работаешь?»
            2. ___ do you work?
            «Where do you work?» = «Где ты работаешь?»
            3. What ___ do you start work?
            «What time» = «Во сколько».
            4. ___ do you finish work?
            «When do you finish work?»
            5. Where do you have ___ ?
            «Have lunch» — без артикля.
            6. How ___ do you go to the gym?
            «How often» — частотность.
            7. What do you do ___ work?
            «After work» = «после работы».
            8. Where does your colleague ___ ?
            В вопросе с «does» — только базовая форма.
